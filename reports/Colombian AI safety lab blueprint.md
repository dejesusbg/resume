# Copy Anthropic's Charter, Own Colombia's Ground Truth

A founder with no money cannot start where Anthropic started. Anthropic's founders had co-authored GPT-3 and the scaling laws, and the company raised about $704 million before it shipped a product. What a founder can copy are the parts of Anthropic that cost almost nothing: a charter that locks in the mission, rigorous evaluations published as a trust signal, and a narrow domain where the lab becomes the name everyone cites. For a Colombian lab that domain should not be "Spanish." Frontier models already handle Spanish well: a May 2026 professional study found text jailbreaks *less* effective in Mexican Spanish than in English. The defensible moat is local ground truth that frontier labs cannot quickly build for themselves. It has three parts: a maintained safety benchmark in Colombian Spanish and in indigenous and Creole languages, built with communities that consent; a living dataset of Latin American AI misuse such as election deepfakes, voice-clone extortion and dual use by armed groups; and standing as the technical evaluator for a country that has no AI Safety Institute and a pending bill that would make MinCiencias (the science ministry) its AI authority. The realistic route over three to five years climbs a ladder that smaller safety organizations have already documented: a volunteer collective, then a $100k–$500k seed team, then a $1.5M–$10M nonprofit, then a public-benefit company that sells evaluations. It never attempts frontier pretraining. First-year money is unusually easy to reach in late 2026. BlueDot grants of up to $20k are decided in about five days. Coefficient Giving's new Project Tailwind offers $200k–$2M pre-seed grants that need no legal entity and accept non-US teams. Spanish AI-safety red-teaming contracts pay $43–47 an hour as bridge income. The main risks are dependence on a few foreign donors, Colombian public funding calls that get cancelled or contested, and a Spanish-language safety gap that keeps shrinking. That last risk is why the moat has to rest on data access and relationships, not on language alone.

## Anthropic's $704 million head start cannot be copied, but its charter can

Anthropic was never a bootstrap story. Former OpenAI leaders, among them Dario Amodei (OpenAI's VP of Research) and Daniela Amodei, founded it in January 2021 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)). Its Series A announcement made the case for its credibility in one line: "The team has previously published research on GPT-3, circuit-based interpretability, and scaling laws" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems)). That **$124M Series A (May 2021)** was led by Skype co-founder Jaan Tallinn, with Dustin Moskovitz, Eric Schmidt and the Center for Emerging Risk Research also investing. It was followed by a **~$580M Series B in 2022** led by Sam Bankman-Fried ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ftx-to-sell-majority-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million)). Claude reached approved users only in **March 2023**; a first version trained in summer 2022 had been held back for safety testing ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)). About $704M and two years came before the first product. The useful detail is who wrote the first checks: mission-aligned philanthropists, the same network that now funds small safety organizations through Coefficient Giving and the Survival and Flourishing Fund. A zero-resource founder cannot match the starting capital, but can reach the same funders.

The cheap parts of Anthropic are its governance and its habits. It described itself as a public benefit corporation from its first funding announcement. In September 2023 it added a **Long-Term Benefit Trust**: five financially disinterested trustees holding a special "Class T" stock that elects a growing share of directors and reaches a **board majority within four years** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust)). The same month it published a **Responsible Scaling Policy**, which ties training and deployment to AI Safety Levels and commits to pausing if safeguards fall behind ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropics-responsible-scaling-policy)). None of this needs capital. Anthropic's commercial moat rests on two further things a small lab can imitate in miniature. One is trust. Menlo Ventures, an Anthropic investor that surveyed about 150 buyers, put Anthropic at **32% of enterprise LLM usage in mid-2025**, ahead of OpenAI's 25%, and at 42% in coding ([Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2025/08/04/anthropic-leads-enterprise-llms-with-32-market-share/)). The other is talent. SignalFire measured **80% two-year retention**, the highest among frontier labs, and credits mission alignment as "not just a hiring filter; it's a retention strategy" ([SignalFire](https://signalfire.com/blog/anthropic-talent-retention)). Scaled down to a tiny lab, these become mission as a retention tool, publication as a trust signal, and dominance of one narrow domain, the way Anthropic won coding.

The smaller safety organizations founded since 2020 show what a realistic climb looks like. Each rung costs roughly ten times the one below it.

| Stage | Money | Team | Documented precedent |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Volunteer collective | $0 cash, donated compute | Volunteers | EleutherAI shipped The Pile and GPT-J within about a year on donated TPUs and GPUs |
| 1. Seed team | $100k–$500k/yr | 2–5 | Timaeus started on a ~$145k regrant and reached 8 staff and ~$2.5M within ~14 months |
| 2. Nonprofit research/evals org | $1.5M–$10M/yr | 8–45 | Apollo Research's $1.54M startup grant; METR at ~45 staff |
| 3. PBC or for-profit safety vendor | $5M–$50M raised | 20+ | Gray Swan: $5.5M seed, then a $40M Series A |
| 4. Frontier model developer | ~$700M before first product | Hundreds | Anthropic |

EleutherAI began in July 2020 as a Discord server whose compute came from Google's TPU Research Cloud and from CoreWeave, where "no money was exchanged" ([EleutherAI](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/)). Timaeus started on a Manifund regrant of about $145k ([Manifund](https://manifund.substack.com/p/reviewing-our-ai-safety-regrants)) and was budgeting about $2.5M for 2025 with eight people ([Timaeus](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/gGAXSfQaiGBCwBJH5/timaeus-in-2024)). Apollo received a **$1,535,480** startup grant from Open Philanthropy ([Open Philanthropy](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/apollo-research-startup-funding)). METR now runs about 45 staff on foundation and government money; it accepts free tokens from AI companies but no cash ([METR](https://metr.org/about)). Gray Swan spun out of Carnegie Mellon, raised a $5.5M seed ([Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/?p=112677)), and closed a **$40M Series A in May 2026**. It reports more than 20 customers and says Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta cite it in their system cards ([Gray Swan](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/gray-swan-announces-series-a)).

Two lessons follow. First, **no organization in this sample has climbed from Stage 0 to Stage 4**. EleutherAI is the only well-documented start from literally zero. It took about 2.7 years to become a funded institute with 20+ staff, and it then moved *away* from training models toward interpretability and evaluation ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/02/stability-ai-hugging-face-and-canva-back-new-ai-research-nonprofit)). Conjecture, the venture-funded follow-on from EleutherAI's founders, pivoted within a year of founding and was "drawing to a close" by March 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy)). For a Colombian founder, the Anthropic-like target in three to five years is therefore Stage 2 or 3: a safety organization that publishes, sells evaluations and adopts Anthropic-style governance. "Building models" should mean post-training open-weight models, not pretraining. One example is a regional safety classifier like AI Singapore's SEA-LION Guard, which is fine-tuned from Gemma ([LLM Reference](https://www.llmreference.com/model/sea-lion-guard-v2-31b)). Second, credentials stand in for capital. Every founder in the sample had a recognizable credential: a Berkeley or CMU PhD, a MATS mentorship, or open-source fame. The exception is Haize Labs, founded by new graduates, which raised its seed seven months after founding at a $100M valuation ([PitchBook](https://www.pitchbook.com/news/articles/general-catalyst-haize-labs-100-million-valuation)), on product traction rather than research reputation. A founder without a credential has to create one, which means a citable result within six to twelve months.

The obvious alternative is to build a Colombian sovereign model. That slot is already taken, and it is the wrong fight. LatamGPT is a 70B-parameter model built on Llama 3.1 and coordinated by Chile's CENIA, with Colombian institutions among its contributors. It launched in February 2026 ([France24/AFP](https://www.france24.com/en/live-news/20260210-latam-gpt-a-latin-american-ai-to-combat-us-centric-bias)) on a modest budget (CAF, the Latin American development bank, committed $250K). Critics warn that "well-funded companies could quickly adapt their own models for Spanish and Portuguese" ([Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/latamgpt-navigates-the-gap-between-regional-aspiration-and-market-realities/)). LatamGPT has published no systematic safety evaluations ([EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)), which makes it a partner and a test subject for a Colombian safety lab, not a rival.

## The real gap is local harm and indigenous languages, not Spanish

The intuitive pitch, that frontier models' guardrails break in Spanish, is mostly wrong. A founder who leads with it will lose credibility with technical funders. Appen's May 2026 study tested Claude Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, Pixtral Large and Qwen Omni in US English and Mexican Spanish, using professional translation and native-speaker annotators. **All five linguistic attack techniques were less effective in Mexican Spanish.** Role-play attacks had about half the odds of success (odds ratio 0.45) ([Emergent Mind](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2605.23157)). Spanish is a high-resource language. The same study, though, found results that do matter. Toxic-image attacks were *more* effective in Spanish (odds ratio 1.58), and **safety rankings did not carry over** between languages: Qwen Omni became the most vulnerable model in Mexican Spanish. Harm is also cultural. In Cohere's Aya red-teaming set, written by paid native speakers, **35% of the 782 Spanish prompts describe local rather than global harms** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.18682v2)). So the defensible claim is not that Spanish is broken. It is that English results do not predict Colombian results, and nobody is measuring the difference.

Institutional testing confirms that last point. The joint multilingual test by the international AI safety institutes (report January 2026) used more than 6,000 translated prompts in ten languages: Cantonese, English, Farsi, French, Japanese, Korean, Kiswahili, Malay, Mandarin and Telugu. It found that safeguard robustness varies by language and that **LLM judges disagree with human judges** outside English. **Spanish and Portuguese were not included** ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15706)). MLCommons' AILuminate safety benchmark covers English, French and Chinese, and its March 2026 roadmap adds no Spanish ([MLCommons](https://mlcommons.org/benchmarks/ailuminate/)). A third-party summary of Anthropic's Mythos Preview system card lists harmlessness testing in seven languages, none of them Spanish or Portuguese ([summary](https://hugobowne.github.io/mythos-preview-model-card/sources/08-appendix)). The safety-institute network, since renamed the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science, has ten members and no Latin American country among them ([CASRAI](https://casrai.org/guides/network-of-ai-safety-institutes)). Meanwhile, Anthropic's Claude Sonnet 4.6 system card is described as the first to include multilingual evaluations ([Anthropic](https://www-cdn.anthropic.com/78073f739564e986ff3e28522761a7a0b4484f84.pdf)). That is a signal that labs value this kind of measurement, and also a warning that they can bring it in-house.

The largest documented gap is in low-resource languages. Yong, Menghini and Bach translated harmful prompts from the AdvBench benchmark and measured how often they got past GPT-4's safeguards. **Guarani, the one South American indigenous language tested, got through 15.96% of the time, against 0.96% for English.** Trying four low-resource languages together succeeded **79.04%** of the time ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.02446v2)). Those numbers come from a 2023 model. Current models have probably improved, but I found no 2025–2026 replication on Latin American indigenous languages. Colombia has **68 native languages: 65 indigenous languages, two Creoles (Palenquero and San Andrés–Providencia Creole) and Romani**, all protected by Ley 1381 de 2010 ([Radio Nacional](https://radionacional.co/cultura/colombia-lenguas-12-de-octubre)). A June 2026 survey of the region found **"no indigenous-language jailbreak or hate-speech benchmark"** anywhere ([EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)). A fresh measurement on 2026 frontier models in Wayuunaiki, Palenquero, San Andrés Creole and other Colombian languages would be a first-of-its-kind result. It would also be cheap, because it runs on API calls. And it matters for catastrophic risk, not just fairness. Translating a request into a low-resource language is a universal jailbreak method. That is the same class of finding Anthropic's model safety bug bounty paid **up to $15,000** for at its 2024 launch, in chemical, biological, radiological, nuclear (CBRN) and cyber domains ([HackerOne](https://www.hackerone.com/blog/anthropic-expands-their-model-safety-bug-bounty-program)). With that framing, one research program can satisfy both funders focused on catastrophic risk and local funders focused on responsible AI.

Regional models are tested even less. Besides LatamGPT's silence on safety, a manual red-teaming study of 670 Spanish and Basque conversations found **inappropriate behavior in 50.6% of conversations with Spain's public ALIA Salamandra model**, against 29.5% for o3-mini ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10192)). Colombia also produces misuse ground truth that no one outside the country can collect:

- **Elections.** Fact-checkers documented AI-manipulated content, including suspected voice cloning, in the second round of the 2026 presidential election ([Lupa](https://lupa.com.ec/verificaciones/elecciones-colombia-2026/)). Kaspersky data cited after the first round says half of Colombians had received false content via WhatsApp or Telegram ([ImpactoTIC](https://impactotic.co/micrositios-tic/soy-digital/desinformacion-electoral-colombia-ia-campanas/)).
- **Fraud and extortion.** Ley 2502 de 2025 made AI-enabled impersonation an aggravating factor in the crime ([Colombia One](https://m.colombiaone.com/wordpress/c/0/i/227042/scam-sounds-your-family-how-ai-voice-cloning-works-and-how-stop-it)). In October 2026 Mexico broke up an extortion ring that cloned the voices of judges and governors ([AFP via Jamaica Observer](https://www.jamaicaobserver.com/2026/10/09/mexico-busts-extortion-network-used-ai-clone-vip-voices/)).
- **Conflict.** In 2023 the Truth Commission handed about **15,000 testimonies and 1,195 reports** to the transitional-justice court (JEP) and the national archive ([Cambio](https://cambiocolombia.com/pais/articulo/2023/5/la-comision-de-la-verdad-entrega-su-archivo-documental-la-jep-y-al-archivo-nacional)).

Colombian volunteers have already prototyped the right kinds of artifacts. Examples include JusticIA, a counterfactual bias benchmark for transitional justice; ColDron, a dataset of 42 drone attacks by armed groups; a reliability evaluation of a customer-service assistant in Colombian Spanish; and structural jailbreaks in Spanish ([Apart Research](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/global-south-ais-hackathon-2026-06-19-to-2026-06-21)). But hackathon projects are not maintained, and an unmaintained benchmark is no moat.

## Four interlocking assets form a moat labs would rather buy than build

Taken one at a time, most candidate moats fail. The scorecard below summarizes the evidence on need, willingness to pay and how easily each could be copied.

| Candidate moat | Evidence of need | Who could copy it, and how fast | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish/LatAm safety evals | Medium: rankings unstable, ~35% local harms, absent from flagship exercises | Appen, Lilt, Mercor, Scale; frontier labs' in-house teams. 3–9 months | Weak alone; useful as the sales channel |
| Indigenous and Creole safety | Highest: Guarani 16% vs English 1% (2023); no benchmarks exist | AmericasNLP groups, CENIA, Cohere's Aya community. 2–5 years | Strong but small market; the research flagship |
| Local misuse evals (elections, extortion, conflict) | High: 2026 election deepfakes, voice-clone fraud | Lab threat-intelligence teams, fact-checkers. 1–3 years | Medium to high, because the data compounds |
| Technical partner to government | High need; weak and fragile willingness to pay | Consultancies, Uniandes, CAF/IDB technical teams. 6–18 months | Medium; first-mover advantage if a law creates an evaluator role |
| Cost and time-zone arbitrage | Not a need in itself | Anyone, immediately | Helpful, but not a moat |
| Biodiversity/agriculture "safe AI" | Low for safety | Any applied-AI group. Months | A distraction for a safety brand |
| Interpretability on small Spanish models | Research value only | Any MATS or Apart researcher. Weeks | Builds credibility and recruiting, not defensibility |

The defensible design combines the middle rows into one organization: **the Andean safety evaluator**. Its core asset is a maintained harm benchmark covering Colombian Spanish plus indigenous and Creole languages, with a public leaderboard of frontier models. Around the benchmark sits a living dataset of regional misuse incidents, and in front of it sits an evaluation role facing the government. Each piece strengthens the others. Incident data keeps the benchmark realistic. Community trust unlocks indigenous-language data. Standing with the Colombian state raises the lab's credibility with frontier labs. Contracts with labs pay for the slow community work.

Frontier labs can add Spanish to their internal test suites and hire Spanish raters through Mercor within weeks. What they cannot get quickly is:

- consented indigenous-language data;
- Colombian incident ground truth;
- working relationships with the transitional-justice court, fact-checkers and the national election registry;
- legal standing as a national evaluator.

Those are things a lab would rather buy or cite than build.

The competition is real but scattered. Commercial vendors already sell Spanish safety labor. Lilt is recruiting "AI Red Team Engineers – Spanish, Latin America" ([Remote Rocketship](https://www.remoterocketship.com/company/lilt/jobs/ai-red-team-engineer-spanish-latin-america-argentina-remote)), and in September 2026 Mercor posted a bilingual Spanish AI-safety red-team contract at **$43–47 an hour** ([Remowork](https://remowork.life/jobs/6a9b13b896fca3a67e925140/bilingual-spanish-generalist-expert-ai-safety)). In Brazil, the University of São Paulo is the only Latin American institution among the 18 university labs in Microsoft's new external red-team alliance ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/07/27/enhancing-ai-security-through-global-ai-red-teaming/)), and researchers in Goiás built MiJaBench, an English–Portuguese adversarial benchmark ([EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)). None of these actors is Colombian or Andean, and none holds data agreements with communities. The closest local actor is **AI Safety Colombia**. It runs Spanish-language seminars on the BlueDot curriculum, an AI-control reading group and Bogotá hubs for Apart hackathons, with support from Apart, BlueDot, Kairos and Coefficient Giving ([AI Safety Colombia](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en)). It should be the new lab's partner and recruiting funnel, not its rival. Arguably, the new lab is the paid, professional layer that this community lacks.

The key commercial insight is that **the gap in the market and the gap in purchasing are the same gap**. Buyers do pay for Spanish safety labor by the hour, and philanthropists have paid for regional field-building. Schmidt Sciences, for example, backed a June 2026 Global South hackathon that drew 1,547 sign-ups and 216 projects, with a dedicated Latin America track ([Apart Research](https://apartresearch.com/sprints/global-south-ais-hackathon-2026-06-19-to-2026-06-21)). But no flagship exercise has bought an institutional evaluation in Spanish or Portuguese, no Latin American government has bought safety evaluations, and no contract value for a regional third-party evaluation has been disclosed. Whether anyone will pay for an institutional product, rather than hourly raters, is the biggest unknown in this plan. In practice, every early paid engagement should be set up so that the lab keeps or publishes its methodology and benchmark, rather than simply renting out people. Otherwise it becomes a staffing agency competing with Mercor.

Ethics is part of the moat, not a footnote. Publishing jailbreak prompts in endangered languages could expose small communities to harm, so community control over the data has to be built in from the start. Masakhane, an African NLP network of more than 1,000 participants, made data sovereignty one of its founding values. It also opposes "parachute research," which treats local people as mere annotators ([Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/)). The AmericasNLP research community treats language sovereignty as an explicit theme and added Wayuunaiki to its shared task in 2025 ([AmericasNLP](https://turing.iimas.unam.mx/americasnlp/AmericasNLP_Call_2025.pdf)). Doing consent agreements, benefit-sharing and responsible disclosure to labs before publication well is expensive, which is exactly why a frontier lab will not do it itself. Two risks remain open. The Spanish gap will keep narrowing as multilingual training improves. And there is no data on how many indigenous-language speakers actually use LLMs, which limits how much misuse risk the indigenous-language work actually addresses.

## Grants that decide in days can fund the first eighteen months

In late 2026, funding is easier for a zero-resource safety founder to reach than it has ever been. Coefficient Giving, Open Philanthropy's new name, says its AI safety spending more than doubled to **$351M in 2025** and is on track to exceed **$1 billion in 2026** ([Coefficient Giving](https://coefficientgiving.org/research/were-urgently-scaling-our-work-on-ai-and-biosecurity/)). Its **Project Tailwind** launched on 9 September 2026 with about $200M to help founders start new safety organizations ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/blog/coefficient-giving-project-tailwind-200-million-launchpad-funds-50-million-october-2-2026-eoi-deadline-general-manager-model-pre-seed-no-legal-entity-strategy)):

- **Grant size:** pre-seed grants run **$200k–$2M**.
- **Eligibility:** they **need no legal entity** and are **open to non-US teams and public-benefit for-profits**. A skilled operator without an ML background can qualify.
- **What it wants:** its list of organizations it would like to see includes a misalignment red team and an independent AI auditing body.

Two filters matter. Tailwind screens out proposals that are unrelated to catastrophic AI risk and proposals that "appear AI-generated" ([Coefficient Giving](https://coefficientgiving.org/tailwind/faqs/)). The founder therefore has to write the pitch personally and lead with the cross-language safeguard-bypass framing, not local fairness.

| Source | Size | Speed | Entity needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| BlueDot Rapid grant | Up to $20k | ~5 days | No |
| BlueDot Career Transition | Up to $200k | ~20 days | No |
| BlueDot Seed | $20k–$200k | ~4 weeks | No |
| EA Funds Transformative AI Fund | $10k–$150k | 6–8 weeks | No |
| GTR AI Safety Fund (€50M pool, "no geographic focus") | Not published | ~7 days to first feedback | No |
| Coefficient Giving Tailwind pre-seed | $200k–$2M | Rolling | No |
| Halcyon Futures nonprofit seed | Up to $3M | Email contact | Organization |
| Survival and Flourishing Fund | Annual rounds ($63M awarded in 2026) | Annual | Organization or fiscal sponsor |

The table draws on the Manifund AI Safety Funder Bulletin of 15 October 2026 ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/ais-funder-bulletin)) and on SFF's eligibility listing ([EffectiveAltruism.org](https://www.effectivealtruism.org/opportunities/recmtTB4opD50U2UF)). None of the fast sources states a US or UK residency requirement, but none explicitly confirms paying Colombian residents either. BlueDot's next Incubator Week takes applications until 11 November 2026 ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/ais-funder-bulletin)). Lightcone Commons, launched in July 2026, accepts applications from individuals and unincorporated groups ([GrantedAI](https://grantedai.com/grants/lightcone-commons-quarterly-grant-rounds-ai-alignment-lightcone-9d4f2b18)) and imports projects posted on Manifund, so a public Manifund page doubles as a Lightcone application.

Several marquee programs are closed to a founder based in Colombia:

- The Anthropic Fellows Program requires US, UK or Canadian work authorization ([Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/anthropic-fellows-program)).
- The US NAIRR pilot is limited to US-based researchers ([Microsoft](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/national-ai-research-resource-nairr-pilot/who-can-apply)).
- The Mercor AI Safety Fund accepts only US or UK applicants ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/ais-funder-bulletin)).
- The UK AISI's Alignment Project is open to any nationality but is currently closed ([AISI](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/grants)).

MATS is the exception worth targeting. It accepts all nationalities, covers the J-1 visa, and for Winter 2027 pays a **$19.2k stipend plus $24k of compute** ([MATS](https://matsprogram.org/apply)). For founders specifically, BlueDot and Entrepreneur First run Fulcrum, a seven-day remote program whose graduates can be considered for BlueDot grants and EF investment ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/MNjFfhmPM8zAhRLYQ/fulcrum-a-new-program-for-ai-safety-entrepreneurship)).

The founder can pay personal expenses with the same skills the lab will later sell. Besides Mercor's $43–47 an hour for Spanish red-team work, Gray Swan's Arena competitions have offered prize pools of up to **$170k**, reached once Google DeepMind and Anthropic joined as sponsors ([Gray Swan](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/google-deepmind-and-anthropic-join-as-agent-red-teaming-challenge-sponsors)). Anthropic's bug bounty is a third source. Colombian AI-training work pays about **$10 an hour** on average ([Noticias RCN](https://www.noticiasrcn.com/economia/hora-trabajo-alimentar-inteligencia-artificial-colombia-1000936)), so these rates are good. But they are bridge income and a recruiting channel, not the business. I did not verify whether HackerOne (which runs Anthropic's bounty) and Gray Swan pay out to winners in Colombia.

Compute is not the binding constraint for this research agenda. Anthropic's External Researcher Access Program grants **$1,000** in API credits for safety research ([Claude Help Center](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9125743-what-is-the-external-researcher-access-program)), OpenAI offers up to $1,000 ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/form/researcher-access-program)), and Google's TPU Research Cloud gives free TPU time to selected researchers ([Google](https://sites.research.google/trc/about/)). On the open market, H100 GPUs averaged **$3.84 per GPU-hour** in September 2026 ([Mercatus](https://www.mercatus-ai.com/blog/gpu-rental-prices)), so one GPU running all year costs about $33.6k.

The research tools are free. Google DeepMind's Gemma Scope 2 releases sparse autoencoders and transcoders for every layer of Gemma 3 up to the 27B model ([Google DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-scope/)). A widely cited paper showed that refusal in 13 open chat models of up to 72B parameters is controlled by a single direction in the model's internal representations ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717)). That method transfers directly to asking why refusals fail in Wayuunaiki or in Caribbean-coast slang. Evaluation, red-teaming and interpretability work on API models and on open-weight models of 7–70B parameters fits within roughly **$5k–$50k a year**. One catch: Anthropic's credit program excludes fine-tuning. Experiments that deliberately fine-tune a model to study misalignment therefore need OpenAI's fine-tuning API or open-weight models. Incorporating unlocks more compute. Microsoft for Startups offers up to about $150k in Azure credits without requiring an investor ([Microsoft](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/answers/questions/5780457/startup-credits)). Google's $200k–$350k tiers require institutional funding ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/startup/ai?hl=it)).

People are the harder constraint. The ILIA 2025 index from CEPAL and CENIA gives Colombia a **perfect 100 on demand for AI training but only 19.6 on producing AI experts** ([El Colombiano](https://www.elcolombiano.com/tecnologia/colombia-lider-ia-latinoamerica-EE30167416)). Real research anchors exist. At Uniandes, the CinfonIA center is led by Pablo Arbeláez, a CVPR and NeurIPS area chair ([Uniandes](https://ingbiomedica.uniandes.edu.co/en/pablo-andr%C3%A9s-arbel%C3%A1ez)). At UNAL, MindLab has been running under Fabio González since 2001 ([MindLab](https://ingenieria.unal.edu.co/mindlab/?cat=6)). Even so, the lab will need to hire engineers and train them into safety researchers rather than find ready-made alignment researchers.

Pay levels set the budget. Local employers pay ML engineers a median of about COP 60M, roughly **$19k a year** ([Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/CO/Job=Machine_Learning_Engineer/Salary)). Nearshore firms advertise senior AI/ML pay near **$105k**, against $222k in the US (a vendor figure) ([Alcor](https://alcor.com/your-own-rd-office-news/nearshoring-software-development-to-colombia/)). Paying research engineers $50k–$90k and residents $24k–$40k beats local offers and competes with nearshore ones. By my arithmetic, a founder, two research engineers and two residents cost roughly **$260k–$575k a year**, including 30–50% Colombian employer costs and compute. That fits inside a Tailwind pre-seed grant.

Argentina's AISAR program shows funders will back a regional pipeline. It is a six-month safety scholarship at the University of Buenos Aires, funded with $77,000 by Open Philanthropy ([Good Ventures](https://www.goodventures.org/?p=5256)), and it pays $600 a month ([BAISH](https://nextbaish.vercel.app/en/activities)). That is an order of magnitude below global programs, so the bar to beat is low. Junior talent tends to drain toward data-labeling platforms. Deel recorded **745% growth in AI-trainer hiring in Colombia**, the fastest in Latin America ([Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/tecnologia/colombia-lidera-contratacion-de-entrenadores-de-inteligencia-artificial-en-latinoamerica-segun-reporte-492607)). That same pool is the lab's best source of evaluators who want a path into research. Colombia sits at UTC-5 year-round, so US East Coast mentors share the working day ([Alcor](https://alcor.com/your-own-rd-office-news/nearshoring-software-development-to-colombia/)). KHIPU 2027 in São Paulo (5–9 April) is the region's main venue for recruiting AI talent ([KHIPU](https://khipu.ai/about-khipu-ai/)).

The strongest counterargument comes from inside the community. Commenters on the 2026 regional map argue that early-career Latin Americans have more impact by "fitting in" at US, UK and EU institutions than by building locally ([EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)). The answer is that the lab should not compete with MATS; it should send people to MATS. It should then offer what the same map says the region lacks: permanent paid positions where work builds on itself instead of restarting with each hackathon.

The region's failures point to the same remedies:

- Condor Camp in Brazil and ITAM's AI Futures Fellowship in Mexico both wound down.
- Roughly **$800k of the region's safety funding came from one donor** ([EA Forum](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)).
- When the FTX Future Fund team resigned in November 2022, it warned that many committed grants would likely go unpaid ([EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HPdWWetJbv4z8eJEe/open-phil-is-seeking-applications-from-grantees-impacted-by)).
- Oxford closed the Future of Humanity Institute in April 2024 after "increasing administrative headwinds" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_Humanity_Institute)).
- Transluce's Manifund page sought $2M and had raised $40k when it closed ([Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/transluce-fund-scalable-democratic-oversight-of-ai)).

The countermeasures are paid core staff; an independent legal home with university affiliations, rather than a university as the only host; and at least three kinds of funding (philanthropy, development money and earned revenue) by the end of year two.

## Colombian law supplies a cheap mission lock, but the state is upside, not runway

Colombian corporate law can approximate Anthropic's trust structure at almost no cost. A Sociedad por Acciones Simplificada (S.A.S.) needs no notary and no minimum capital. Under Ley 1258 de 2008, its bylaws can ([Supersociedades](https://www.supersociedades.gov.co/documents/107391/159032/Ley+1258+del+5+de+diciembre+de+2008.pdf/32b063da-d7cc-c55f-2770-1560116b4745)):

- create share classes with **multiple votes** (art. 11);
- allow shares to be held in a **fiducia mercantil**, a commercial trust (art. 12);
- **prohibit share transfers for up to ten years** (art. 13);
- void any transfer that breaches the bylaws (art. 15).

Shareholder agreements are binding for ten years and can be renewed ([Asuntos Legales](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/analisis/andres-felipe-quiroga-511176/acuerdos-de-accionistas-en-sociedades-sas-2395746)). A plausible Colombian version of Anthropic's Class T stock would be a mission share class with the right to elect board members, held by a trust company whose trust deed obliges it to vote in line with the safety mission. This is my inference: there is no precedent, and nobody has tested whether a Colombian trust company would accept such a mandate. Adding the BIC (benefit corporation) status under Ley 1901 de 2018 writes a collective-benefit purpose into the bylaws but brings **no special tax treatment** ([ANLA](https://www.anla.gov.co/wanla/eureka/normativa/leyes/ley-1901-de-2018-sociedades-comerciales-de-beneficio-e-interes-colectivo-bic)). It is a signal, not a legal shield.

The nonprofit arm follows its own rules. A foundation or association in the special tax regime (Régimen Tributario Especial) pays 20% tax only on surplus it does not reinvest ([Estatuto Tributario](https://estatuto.co/356)), and donors get a 25% income-tax discount ([Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/descuento-tributario-por-donaciones-a-entidades-calificadas-en-el-regimen-de-tributacion-especial/)). However, the tax authority's Concepto 153 de 2025 bars these nonprofits from claiming research-and-development tax discounts and credits themselves ([INCP](https://incp.org.co/publicaciones/infoincp-publicaciones/impuestos/2025/03/esal-y-mipymes-en-el-rst-no-pueden-acceder-a-incentivos-tributarios-en-ciencia-tecnologia-e-innovacion/)). The workaround is to get MinCiencias to recognize the nonprofit as a research center. That takes **90–120 business days** ([MinCiencias](https://minciencias.gov.co/portafolio/reconocimiento_de_actores)) and is the gate to most incentives ([MinCiencias](https://minciencias.gov.co/viceministerios/conocimiento/direccion_transferencia/beneficios-tributarios/cuales-son)):

- Recognized centers can import equipment for qualifying projects **free of VAT**.
- Taxable companies that co-invest with a recognized center can claim a 30% tax discount or a 50% tax credit.
- PhD salaries earn a 30% tax discount.

Apollo Research shows the order of moves. It began as a nonprofit and told funders early that it might convert. In January 2026 it became a public benefit corporation with independent "mission seats" on its board, buying the nonprofit's intellectual property at an outside valuation ([Apollo Research](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-research-is-becoming-a-pbc/)). A US (Delaware) parent company only makes sense once US venture money is actually on the table. Y Combinator's standard $500k deal ([YC](https://ycombinator.com/deal)) goes only to companies incorporated in the US, the Cayman Islands or Singapore; Canada was added back after a backlash ([BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-a-small-change-to-y-combinators-terms-sent-waves-through-canadian-tech/)). Any foreign parent must also register its investment with Banco de la República, Colombia's central bank ([Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/files/2026/09/16/RESUMEN-POR-CAPITULOS_Guia-Legal-de-Inversion-2026-1-.docx.pdf)).

Colombia's physical and legal infrastructure pushes the lab toward evaluation and away from training, which suits the strategy:

- **Cloud and power.** Oracle runs Colombia's only hyperscale cloud region ([Oracle](https://blogs.oracle.com/oracle-latinoamerica/oracle-abre-la-primera-region-de-nube-publica-a-hiperscala-en-colombia-bogota)); a secondary source says AWS, Google Cloud and Azure have none ([zop.dev](https://zop.dev/cloud-cost-optimization/colombia)). Industrial electricity averaged **$0.224/kWh in 2025, the second-highest in Latin America** ([El Oriente](https://www.eloriente.com/articulo/colombia-registra-una-de-las-tarifas-electricas-mas-altas-en-america-latina/57741)). The lab should rent US cloud GPUs, not buy its own.
- **US export controls** do not block Colombia. The US rescinded its AI Diffusion Rule in May 2025 ([BIS](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-rescinds-biden-era-artificial-intelligence-diffusion-rule-strengthens-chip-related)). Licenses are now required for advanced chips going to entities headquartered in arms-embargoed countries such as China, or in Macau, wherever those entities operate ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/06/bis-publishes-guidance-license-requirements-advanced-computing-items)), so such investors should be kept off the cap table.
- **Data and copyright law** is stricter than it looks. A 2024 circular from the data-protection authority (SIC Circular 002) requires a privacy impact assessment before designing high-risk AI processing, and says internet data is not public just because it is accessible ([SIC](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/normativa/Circular%20Externa%20No.%20002%20del%2021%20de%20agosto%20de%202024.pdf)). Colombian copyright law has no exception for text and data mining ([EAFIT](https://ligeia.eafit.edu.co/bitstreams/2ffc64e2-61ab-4e9e-86c8-55575c2d461c/download)). Both rules favor consent-based evaluation datasets over scraped training corpora.
- **Visas for foreign mentors.** The digital-nomad visa requires only about $1,660 a month in income but forbids being paid by Colombian entities ([ColombiaMove](https://colombiamove.com/blog/es/colombia-digital-nomad-visa-income-requirement-2026/)). Visiting researchers should therefore be paid by a foreign entity or sponsored for a worker visa.

The Colombian state is a future customer, not a funder to rely on. Colombia has **no AI law**. The government's 2025 AI bill died without a debate on 19 June 2026 ([Contexto](https://contextomedia.com/pagara-colombia-el-precio-de-no-tener-una-ley-para-la-inteligencia-artificial/)). Its replacement, bill PL 025/2026, would make **MinCiencias the national AI authority**, with EU-style risk tiers and fines of up to 3,000 monthly minimum wages. As of mid-October it had not had its first debate, and the current legislative session ends on 20 June 2027 ([Porcontar](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/)). The national AI policy, CONPES 4144, budgets COP 479,273 million through 2030, but only **COP 7,759 million, about 1.6%, for risk mitigation** ([Porcontar](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/)). Public calls have also been unreliable. MinCiencias's ColombIA Inteligente 2026 funding call was terminated in August after failures in its platform and evaluation process ([El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/ciencia/minciencias-acabo-la-convocatoria-colombia-inteligente-2026-por-fallas-en-el-proceso/)). A COP 630 billion call for AI infrastructure went to InterNexa, the only qualified bidder, and the Inspector General's office (Procuraduría) then flagged "serias inconsistencias" ([El Espectador](https://www.elespectador.com/ciencia/procuraduria-inconsistencias-en-convocatoria-de-minciencias-por-cop-630000-millones/)). One outlet describes the new government as ideologically aligned with Donald Trump and sees few signs that it will push the more socially focused AI proposals ([Contexto](https://contextomedia.com/pagara-colombia-el-precio-de-no-tener-una-ley-para-la-inteligencia-artificial/)).

The opening comes from the structure, not from the budget. If PL 025 passes, MinCiencias will have authority but no capacity to evaluate models. The only organization ready to supply Spanish and indigenous-language test suites will be the one that built them. Internationally, the lab can act as a bridge, because the new Global South Network on AI Safety & Evaluation co-launched by IDRC (Canada's International Development Research Centre) ([IDRC](https://idrc-crdi.ca/en/news/idrc-india-ai-impact-summit-2026-driving-responsible-ai-global-impact)) exists and the international measurement network has no Latin American member.

Put in order, the pieces give a concrete sequence. The table below is my synthesis of the evidence above, not a documented precedent.

| Window | Legal form | Money | Deliverable | Moat asset built |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Months 0–3 | None; founder applies as an individual | BlueDot Rapid, lab API credits, bounty and red-team income | 2026 re-measurement of how often safeguards are bypassed in Colombian Spanish, Creoles and indigenous languages, disclosed to labs before publication | First result and credential |
| Months 3–12 | Fiscal sponsor, then a Colombian nonprofit | Tailwind pre-seed, BlueDot Seed, Transformative AI Fund, GTR, Lightcone | Open benchmark v1, built with AI Safety Colombia volunteers and community partners | Consent and data-governance agreements |
| Years 1–2 | Nonprofit plus recognition by MinCiencias as a research center | SFF, Halcyon, IDRC, MinCiencias 2027 calls, reopened AISI rounds | Maintained leaderboard; dataset of misuse incidents; red-teaming of LatamGPT with CENIA | Incident network and institutional standing |
| Years 2–4 | Add an S.A.S. with BIC status and mission shares; add a Delaware PBC parent only if raising US venture money | Evaluation contracts with labs, platforms and banks; safety-focused investors | Paid evaluations; technical support to the AI authority; a regional safety-classifier model | Revenue that funds the community work |

## Conclusion

Anthropic was founded on the idea that you have to build frontier models in order to make them safe. A Colombian lab should flip that idea: evaluate everyone else's models in the places no one else can reach. Evaluation is the one role in AI safety where being small, local and Spanish-speaking helps instead of hurting, because the scarce input is access to communities, incidents and institutions, not compute. On that model, the lab's equivalent of Claude is a maintained benchmark and incident dataset. Its equivalent of Anthropic's enterprise trust is being cited in frontier system cards the way Gray Swan is, and being the first call when Colombia's AI authority needs a test suite.

The window is also limited in time, and that should set the founder's pace. The gaps that make this moat possible describe 2026: Spanish and Portuguese missing from institutional test suites, no Latin American safety institute, no indigenous-language benchmark, and a regional model that nobody has tested. These gaps will close as labs expand multilingual evaluation and as Colombia eventually passes an AI law. Whoever ships the first maintained benchmark, with community agreements already in place, becomes the default reference. Anyone who arrives later has to spend the two to five years of trust-building that copying it would take. The weakest link in the plan is whether anyone will pay institutional rates, rather than hourly rates, for regional evaluation. The first eighteen months, funded by grants that need no legal entity, should be designed to answer that question cheaply before the lab commits to a corporate structure that depends on the answer.
