Scope note: Colombia first, wider Latin America as context. All data points are dated where the source gave a date. Currency conversions marked "(calc.)" are my own arithmetic using the Colombian official rate (TRM) of COP 3,163.76 per USD on 21 Oct 2026 ([Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/economia/finanzas/precio-del-dolar-hoy-en-colombia-la-divisa-inicia-cerca-de-3-160-este-21-de-octubre-504832)). ## 1. Colombian universities, research groups, and the size and quality of the talent pool

### Takeaway
Colombia has a few real research anchors: Uniandes' CinfonIA under Pablo Arbeláez, whose work is CVPR/NeurIPS-calibre, and UNAL's MindLab under Fabio González, running since 2001. Beyond them it has a wide but shallow base: about 52 AI research groups, and an estimated 5,000 to 13,000 systems/informatics engineering graduates a year depending on definition. CEPAL's index scores Colombia at the top of the region for AI *training demand* but weak on *expert-level* (PhD) formation. In practice a new lab can hire capable engineers but will find very few senior researchers in the country.

### Cited Findings
**National indicators**
- ILIA 2025, the Latin American AI index from CEPAL and CENIA, ranks Colombia **4th of 19 countries with 55.84/100**, behind Chile, Brazil and Uruguay. On the "enabling factors" dimension (infrastructure, data, talent) it ranks **7th**. — [MinTIC](https://mintic.gov.co/portal/inicio/Sala-de-prensa/Noticias/416782:Colombia-asciende-al-cuarto-lugar-en-el-Indice-Latinoamericano-de-Inteligencia-Artificial-de-la-CEPAL); [ImpactoTIC](https://impactotic.co/inteligencia-artificial/colombia-asciende-al-cuarto-lugar-en-indice-latinoamericano-de-inteligencia-artificial-ilia-2025/)

### Inferences
- Realistic senior hiring in Colombia comes down to a handful of PI-level people at Uniandes and UNAL, plus diaspora returnees. The deep pool is engineers and master's-level ML practitioners. A lab should expect to hire *engineers* locally and *train* them into safety research, rather than to hire ready-made alignment researchers.
- The "100 on training demand, 19.6 on expert formation" split in ILIA is the key structural fact: lots of demand for AI education, few PhDs. Funded PhD-equivalent residencies or fellowships would fill a gap no one else in Colombia is filling.
- Uniandes is the natural physical anchor. It already hosts AI Safety Colombia's in-person seminar sessions, an AI Safety student group, CinfonIA, and AI-governance faculty (Gutiérrez). Medellín (EAFIT, Ruta N/C4IR) is the secondary hub.

### Gaps
- I found no CSRankings, DBLP or Scholar count of Colombian-affiliated papers at NeurIPS, ICML or ACL. No country-level authorship breakdown for these venues turned up. This needs a manual DBLP affiliation query.
- I found no authoritative annual SNIES/OLE series for CS/systems graduates or AI PhD graduates. The figures above use inconsistent definitions and years.
- No specific evidence turned up on AI/NLP groups at Universidad del Valle, UIS, Universidad del Rosario or Universidad del Norte, beyond Niebles' past appointment. Minciencias GrupLAC would have the group classifications.
- I did not confirm Uniandes or UNAL PhD cohort sizes in AI.

## 2. Colombian and LatAm diaspora at frontier labs; Khipu and LatinX in AI as networks

### Takeaway
Searches turned up no publicly identifiable Colombians at Anthropic, OpenAI or Google DeepMind. The best-documented senior Colombian in frontier-adjacent AI is Juan Carlos Niebles (Salesforce AI Research / Stanford). Regional networking runs through KHIPU, which works like Latin America's Deep Learning Indaba, and the LatinX in AI affinity events at NeurIPS. Both are good recruiting and diaspora channels for a new lab.

### Cited Findings
- **Juan Carlos Niebles** is described by Salesforce as "a prominent researcher of Colombian origin". He is Director of Research at Salesforce AI Research (since 2021), Adjunct Professor of CS at Stanford, and co-director of the Stanford Vision and Learning Lab. He serves on the AI Index Steering Committee, is Curriculum Director of Stanford-AI4ALL, and is an Area Chair for CVPR, ICCV and ECCV. He was a Fulbright scholar (2005). — [Salesforce](https://www.salesforce.com/blog/celebrating-juan-carlos-niebles/); [Stanford ExploreCourses](https://explorecourses.stanford.edu/instructor/jniebles)
- Targeted searches (English and Spanish) for Colombian-born researchers at OpenAI, DeepMind, Meta or Anthropic returned **no public results**. The searches surfaced only general lab-poaching news. — [search results e.g. Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/negocios/empresas/los-tres-fichajes-estrella-de-meta-para-su-proyecto-de-superinteligencia-634040)
- UNAL alumni appear as ML faculty and PhD students at US universities (e.g. Drexel, Northeastern), going by their public academic profiles. This points to a US academic diaspora pipeline. — [Drexel profile](https://researchdiscovery.drexel.edu/esploro/profile/joseph_gallego_mejia); [Northeastern student site](https://alceballosa.github.io)
- The LatAm diaspora in AI safety and governance, as listed by the June 2026 EA Forum map, includes Renan Araújo (IAPS), Jonathan Dannevig (ERA), Clarissa Rios Rojas (CSER) and Caio Vieira Machado (The Future Society). Most are Brazilian or Peruvian; none is identified as Colombian. — [EA Forum map, 18 Jun 2026](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)
- **KHIPU** began in 2019 as a biennial meeting: 2019 Montevideo, 2021 online, 2023 Montevideo, 2025 Santiago, with **2027 in São Paulo (5–9 April 2027, USP)**. Each edition draws about **500 participants** from **over 1,000 applicants**. It reports **1,200+ trained** and **520+ travel grants** across editions. — [KHIPU About](https://khipu.ai/about-khipu-ai/)
- KHIPU 2025 (10–14 Mar 2025, PUC Chile, Santiago) drew **"more than 800"** students, researchers and startup/industry people according to Inria Chile. This conflicts with KHIPU's own "about 500 per edition", probably because Inria counts total attendance including non-selected or public sessions. Google was a Platinum sponsor. — [Inria Chile](https://inria.cl/en/role-inria-and-academia-industry-collaboration-artificial-intelligence-and-outcomes-oceania-inria); [Google at KHIPU 2025](https://research.google/conferences-and-events/google-at-khipu-2025)
- **KHIPU.AI – Latin American AI Institute** is a US 501(c)(3) established in 2024. Its board is Pablo Sprechmann, Meire Fortunato, Stephanie Hughes-Fitt and Federico Lecumberry. **KHIPUx** (since 2024) funds locally organized workshops and schools: **30+ events in 10 countries** so far, with a 2026–27 call open. — [KHIPU About](https://khipu.ai/about-khipu-ai/); [KHIPUx 2026-2027](https://khipu.ai/khipux-2026-2027/)
- **LatinX in AI** ran a NeurIPS 2025 affinity event (2 Dec 2025, San Diego) and co-hosted a joint WiML / LatinX in AI / Queer in AI affinity workshop (30 Nov 2025). NeurIPS 2025 also had a **Mexico City satellite**, where WiML and LXAI held a joint networking reception. — [NeurIPS 2025 LXAI](https://nips.cc/virtual/2025/133982); [NeurIPS joint workshop](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2025/133292); [Luma CDMX reception](https://luma.com/4g8258pe)

### Inferences
- The most practical diaspora strategy is to build an explicit "returnee and remote affiliate" track: adjunct or advisory roles for Colombian-origin researchers in the US, such as Niebles-type profiles, plus recruiting at KHIPU 2027 (São Paulo) and NeurIPS LXAI events. A KHIPUx grant could co-fund a Bogotá "AI safety school" under KHIPU's brand. Their stated mission covers foundation models and RL training, not safety specifically.
- The absence of visible Colombians at frontier labs is partly a data artifact, since nationality is rarely public. Even so, it suggests the lab can't count on an existing frontier-lab Colombian network for mentors. It will need to import mentors, remotely or via visas.

### Gaps
- No public, aggregate count of Colombians or Latin Americans at frontier labs exists that I could find. LinkedIn-based estimates would be needed and fall outside "publicly available aggregate" data.
- I could not access the KHIPU 2023 outcomes report (PDF over 10 MB) for applicant breakdown by country, including Colombian share.
- I found no LatinX in AI membership size or country-breakdown statistics.

## 3. Existing AI safety and AI governance community in Colombia and Latin America

### Takeaway
Colombia already has a small but real AI safety community. **AI Safety Colombia** (Bogotá) runs Spanish-language BlueDot-curriculum seminars, an AI-control reading group, monthly research talks and Apart Research hackathon hubs. It is backed by Apart, BlueDot, Kairos and Coefficient Giving, and has a few papers out. Regionally, the strongest group is Buenos Aires' BAISH, which has a couple hundred members. The broader LatAm field is "thin": two flagship pipelines have shut down, no university technical alignment lab exists anywhere in the region, there is no AISI, and roughly USD 800k of funding comes almost entirely from one donor. That leaves an open niche for a permanent Colombian lab.

### Cited Findings
**AI Safety Colombia (Bogotá)**
- AI Safety Colombia's tagline is "From Colombia to the frontier of AI safety". Its programs are:
  - 5–6-week **seminars** reading **BlueDot** courses, facilitated in Spanish: a Technical AI Safety track and a Future and Risks of AI track.
  - **Weekend sprints/hackathons with Apart Research**, run from a Bogotá in-person hub.
  - A biweekly **AI-control reading group** with no prerequisites.
  - **Monthly research talks**.
  - Dinners and panels, e.g. "De OpenAI a Bogotá" and "Colombia e IA: la política detrás de la tecnología".

  Listed partners are **Apart Research, BlueDot Impact, Kairos, and Coefficient Giving**. — [AI Safety Colombia](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en)
- The current **Technical AI Safety Seminar** runs **19 Oct to 29 Nov 2026**. It is hybrid, with in-person sessions at **Universidad de los Andes**, and free. AI Safety Colombia also offers mentorship and support for university AI safety groups. — [Technical seminar page](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en/seminars/technical) (via search summary)
- Outputs listed by AI Safety Colombia:
  - (a) Dec 2025 working paper "AI Benefit-Sharing Framework: Balancing Access and Safety" with the **Oxford Martin AI Governance Initiative**, by Camila Beltran and 18 co-authors.

A 2025 articulation report and an AI capabilities diagnostic for Medellín exist. — [Ruta N C4IR report](https://rutanmedellin.org/hubfs/ReporteArticulacionC4irWef.pdf?hsLang=es); [Medellín Alcaldía](https://www.medellin.gov.co/es/sala-de-prensa/noticias/el-centro-para-la-cuarta-revolucion-industrial-para-colombia-vuelve-a-tener-sede-en-medellin/); [Ruta N AI diagnostic](https://rutanmedellin.org/hubfs/DiagnoosticoCapacidadesOportunidadesIA.pdf?hsLang=es)
- **CENIA (Chile)** co-produces ILIA with CEPAL and coordinates LatamGPT. MinTIC has publicized ILIA–CENIA collaboration. — [MinTIC](https://mintic.gov.co/portal/inicio/Sala-de-prensa/Noticias/437885:ILIA-x-CENIA-la-colaboracion-latinoamericana-clave-para-el-desarrollo-de-la-Inteligencia-Artificial-en-la-region)

### Inferences
- A new lab does not start from zero. The lab should partner with AI Safety Colombia rather than duplicate it. AI Safety Colombia handles the top of the funnel (seminars, reading groups, hackathons); the lab supplies what the EA Forum map says is missing everywhere in LatAm: paid, permanent research positions where hackathon outputs can "compound".
- There is a clear niche moat. The region needs Spanish-language and indigenous-language safety evaluations, LatamGPT red-teaming, and evaluations tied to regional threats (cybercrime, disinformation, bio). This is the LatAm equivalent of Masakhane's language moat, and no one currently owns it.
- Funding concentration (one donor, about USD 800k) is a fragility. A lab should seek diversified funding from the outset: KHIPUx, Google/Microsoft academic programs, Minciencias/CONPES lines, and frontier-lab grant programs.
- The governance community (Gutiérrez, Karisma, C4IR) is rights- and deployment-harm-focused. Framing safety work as evaluations and incident response, rather than "x-risk", will make coalition-building easier. The Sept 2026 incident-response sprint is a template for this.

## 4. Salary benchmarks, cost of living, remote-employer competition, and time zone

### Takeaway
There are two labor markets. **Local employers** pay ML engineers roughly **USD 10k–36k (median about USD 19k)**. **Foreign and nearshore employers** pay senior AI/ML engineers roughly **USD 60k–105k**, about 35–55% of US base pay. A Bogotá or Medellín professional lifestyle costs about USD 1,000–2,500 a month. The peso strengthened sharply in 2026 (TRM about 3,160 in Oct 2026), so local salaries now cost more in USD terms. Competition comes from nearshore firms (EPAM, Endava, Globant) and AI-data platforms (Outlier/Scale, Mercor). Colombia's fixed UTC-5 means full overlap with US Eastern hours.

- **Suggested salary bands for a safety lab (calc., hedged).** To beat nearshore offers and keep people from leaving for US remote roles, a lab likely needs these bands:
  - Research engineers: about **USD 50k–90k** base.
  - Senior and research-lead hires: **USD 100k–150k**.
  - Fellows and residents: **USD 24k–40k** stipends. This is already above the local ML median of about USD 19k and well above AI-data gig pay.

  Add about 30–50% for Colombian employer costs if hiring locally rather than via an employer of record.
- Peso appreciation (about 11% YTD in 2026) and the 23% minimum-wage hike raise the USD cost of COP-denominated salaries and local overhead. USD-denominated pay is still attractive to staff, but budgets set in 2025 are now light.
- AI-data platforms are the biggest drain on *junior* technical talent: fast, flexible pay of USD 10–32/hr, with Colombia leading LatAm in growth. The lab can turn this into an advantage by recruiting evaluators and red-teamers from that pool and offering a path into research.
- The UTC-5 overlap makes "Colombian lab + US/Canada mentors" collaboration very cheap. This is a concrete advantage over African or Asian Global South hubs.

## 5. Proven field-building tactics (Apart, BlueDot, MATS, Cohere Labs/Aya, EleutherAI, Masakhane)

### Takeaway
Successful volunteer-to-research communities share five traits:
1. A near-zero-barrier entry ramp (open Discord/Slack, Colab notebooks, no prerequisites).
2. Short, structured output loops (weekend hackathons, 5-week courses, scholar programs with mentors).
3. A concrete shared artifact: a model, a dataset or a benchmark.
4. Publication paths that credit contributors.
5. A mission framing with local ownership, as in Masakhane's "language moat" and anti-"parachute research" stance.

They then formalize into a funded nonprofit with paid core staff.

### Cited Findings
**Masakhane (African NLP)**
- Masakhane began at **Deep Learning Indaba 2019**, founded by Jade Abbott. It describes itself as an "open-source, continent-wide, distributed, online research effort". — [Stellenbosch](https://ee.sun.ac.za/participatory-research-for-low-resourced-machine-translation-a-case-study-in-african-languages); [arXiv 2003.11529](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.11529)
- Mechanics:
  - Weekly online meetings, talks and socials.
  - Slack and a Google Group.
  - **No prerequisites beyond a Code of Conduct**.
  - Starter **Colab notebooks on Joey NMT**.

  Scale: **1,000+ participants from 30 African countries**. By Feb 2020, **35 contributors had published MT results for 38 languages** on GitHub. — [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/)
- Values: Ubuntu, African ownership, data sovereignty, reproducibility, and explicit opposition to **"parachute research"**, i.e. Africans as mere data providers or annotators. Slogan: "The 4th Industrial revolution in Africa cannot take place in English." — [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/)
- The **EMNLP Findings 2020** participatory-research paper produced **MT benchmarks for 30+ languages, human evaluation for a third of them**, and research contributions from people **without formal training**. It won the **2020 Wikimedia Foundation Research Award**. The barriers it set out to address were lack of community, language complexity, poor reproducibility and no benchmarks. — [Stellenbosch](https://ee.sun.ac.za/participatory-research-for-low-resourced-machine-translation-a-case-study-in-african-languages); [InstaDeep](https://www.instadeep.com/?p=1205)
- Spinoff projects include MasakhaNER, LAFAND-MT, MMT-Africa and question answering. Venues: AfricaNLP@ICLR, WiNLP@ACL, COLING, EMNLP Findings, and a WMT keynote. — [Masakhane](https://www.masakhane.io/)

**EleutherAI**
- EleutherAI started as a Discord server on **7 Jul 2020** (originally "LibreAI") to replicate GPT-3 in the open. It had **no formal membership** (participation by self-identification) and no funding at first. Projects were **bottom-up**: a member proposes, recruits volunteers and finds compute. Discussion is public, which acts as onboarding. Spinoffs include OpenBioML. — [EleutherAI: Going Beyond "Open Science" (arXiv 2210.06413)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.06413.pdf); [EleutherAI blog Year One](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one)
- It **incorporated as a nonprofit in early 2023**. It now has about **two dozen full- and part-time staff plus about a dozen regular volunteers**. As of 2025 it no longer trains its own frontier models; it focuses on datasets, research and policy. — [EleutherAI About](https://www.eleuther.ai/about); [Wikipedia](https://www.wikipedia.com/wiki/EleutherAI)

**Cohere Labs (formerly Cohere For AI) / Aya**
- **Aya** involved **3,000+ independent researchers in 119 countries**. It grew out of Cohere For AI's **Open Science community** (100+ countries), which supports independent researchers and offers **subsidized API access**. The output was the **Aya model (101 languages, Feb 2024)** and the largest multilingual instruction dataset at the time (**513M data points, 114 languages**), followed by Aya 23, Aya Expanse and Aya Vision as open weights. The project start is dated Jan 2023 in some sources; the "largest open-science effort" claim is Cohere's own. — [Aya at a glance](https://cohere.com/research/aya/aya-at-a-glance.pdf); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/02/13/cohere-ai-unveils-aya-multilingual-open-source-ai-101-languages/); [Expedition Aya FAQ](https://sites.google.com/cohere.com/expedition-aya/faq)

**Apart Research**
- Apart's self-reported totals: **6,000+ participants, 55 events, 200+ locations, 22 peer-reviewed papers** at top venues. A third-party tally gives 3,000+ participants, 42+ sprints and 22 papers. One count includes **2 ICLR 2025 orals**. — [Apart Meetup](https://www.meetup.com/apartresearch); [Longterm Wiki](https://longterm-wiki.vercel.app/organizations/apart-research)
- An earlier Alignment Jam series had **900+ participants across 13 hackathons, producing 168 projects**. — [Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/run-five-international-hackathons-on-ai-safety-research)
- Apart's **local-hub model** (global sprint plus in-person nodes) is exactly what AI Safety Colombia uses in Bogotá. It is also BAISH's main paper venue. — [AI Safety Colombia sprint](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en/sprint); [EA Forum map](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)

**BlueDot Impact**
- Growth: **400** in its first AI safety course, **900 trained in 2023**, **3,500 in 2024**. Secondary sources say 7,000+ since 2022, and one says 10,000+. — [BlueDot About](https://bluedot.org/about); [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/companies/bluedot_impact)
- BlueDot lets local groups run its curriculum with local facilitators (AI Safety Colombia does this in Spanish). **Rapid Grants** have paid out USD 1.4M with about 3-day decisions. — [AI Safety Colombia](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en); [Sprint page](https://aisafetycolombia.org/en/sprint)

**MATS**
- **About 80% of pre-2025 alumni are still working in AI safety** (survey of 242 of 292, about 83% coverage). Of alumni, 34% are at nonprofits, 27% at for-profits and 6% in government, and **10% co-founded an AI safety startup or team**. This comes from an informal shortform by MATS's Ryan Kidd. Earlier surveys: 49% working on alignment (72 alumni, 2021–23 cohorts), and 61% full-time on safety within 5 months (Spring 2024 extension). — [Ryan Kidd shortform](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/tPjAgWpsQrveFECWP/ryan-kidd-s-shortform); [MATS Alumni Impact Analysis](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/jeBkx6agMuBCQW94C/mats-alumni-impact-analysis)

**Regional analogs**
- **KHIPU ≈ Deep Learning Indaba.** It has a 501(c)(3) institute and KHIPUx micro-grants (30+ local events in 10 countries) for local schools and workshops. — [KHIPU](https://khipu.ai/about-khipu-ai/)
- **BAISH** shows the LatAm safety-specific model:
  - BlueDot courses
  - weekly re-implementations
  - Apart sprints
  - a six-month paid scholarship (AISAR)

  — [EA Forum map](https://ea.greaterwrong.com/posts/3sL6iB6WzKHJciwta/latin-america-in-search-of-its-stake-in-ai-safety-a-map)

### Inferences
**A playbook for a Colombian lab, synthesized from the above:**
1. **Funnel.** Sponsor and expand AI Safety Colombia's Spanish BlueDot seminars at Uniandes, UNAL, EAFIT and La Sabana (top of funnel). Then run Apart-hub sprints every quarter (activation). Then a paid 3–6-month residency, MATS- or AISAR-style, with US-time-zone mentors (conversion). Then full-time research-engineer roles (retention).
2. **Masakhane-style moat.** Build an open, participatory "Spanish and indigenous-language safety evals" project: jailbreak and refusal benchmarks, LatamGPT red-teaming, and regional threat evals. Use starter Colab notebooks and a code of conduct as the only gate. Credit every contributor on papers. Frame it as LatAm-owned, not "parachute" safety research. This directly fills gaps the EA Forum map names.
3. **EleutherAI-style openness.** Run a public Discord/Slack with bottom-up project proposals and compute grants. Publicly visible work makes onboarding self-serve.
4. **Cohere-style leverage.** Provide subsidized API and compute access and a single flagship shared artifact (one benchmark or dataset), so hundreds of part-time contributors can coordinate.
5. **Anchor and formalize early.** The region's failures (Condor Camp, AI Futures Fellowship) came from volunteer-only structures and single-donor dependence. Paid core staff, a university affiliation (e.g. CinfonIA or the Uniandes School of Government), and multiple funders are the counter-measures.
6. **Use regional networks for distribution.** KHIPU 2027 (São Paulo), KHIPUx grants, LatinX in AI at NeurIPS, and the NeurIPS Mexico City satellite.

### Gaps
- I did not obtain the full Masakhane community-methodology text (only abstracts and site), nor a quantitative Masakhane retention or attrition analysis.
- Apart and BlueDot participant figures disagree across sources, and no LatAm or country breakdown exists publicly.
- I found no retrospective on why Condor Camp or the AI Futures Fellowship wound down, beyond the map's summary.

## 6. Visa and immigration routes; ease of remote collaboration

### Takeaway
Under Resolución 5477 de 2022, Colombia offers a **V digital-nomad visa** valid up to 2 years, with a low income threshold (about USD 1,660/month in 2026), but holders **cannot be paid by Colombian entities**. Foreign researchers employed by a Colombian-incorporated lab would instead need an **M Trabajador** (worker) visa. A US-entity lab paying foreigners remotely could use the nomad route. Reports indicate Cancillería has tightened its interpretation since late 2025.

### Cited Findings
- **Resolución 5477 de 2022** (in force about 21–22 Oct 2022) keeps three visa types: **V (visitor), M (migrant), R (resident)**. New V subcategories include **digital nomad**. — [Cancillería comparison table](https://cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/DOCUMENTOS-2026/cuadro-comparativo-de-reclasificacion-de-visas-con-la-resolucion-5477-2022.pdf); [Resolución 5477 text](https://www.cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/Normograma/docs/pdf/resolucion_minrelaciones_5477_2022.pdf); [Asuntos Legales, Aug 2026](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/consultorio/nuevo-regimen-migratorio-en-colombia-3495843)
- **V Nómada Digital:**
  - Valid **up to 2 years**; beneficiaries (dependents) can be included.
  - **2026 income threshold: 3x minimum wage = COP 5,252,715/month**, about USD 1,640 at the Jul 2026 rate. That is about **USD 1,660 at the 21 Oct 2026 TRM** (calc.).
  - Evidence: 3 months of bank statements plus an employer, client or project letter.
  - Mandatory health and accident insurance in Colombia.
  - **No paid activity for Colombia-domiciled persons or companies.**
  - Government fee about USD 178 if approved, plus about USD 79 for the cédula de extranjería.

  (Source dated 30 Jul 2026, updated 15 Oct 2026.) — [ColombiaMove](https://colombiamove.com/blog/es/colombia-digital-nomad-visa-income-requirement-2026/)
- Other guides cite an application fee of about **USD 54–56**, probably the study fee as opposed to the issuance fee. They also note **tighter Cancillería interpretation of who qualifies as a digital worker since late 2025**, and **more rejections in 2024–2025**. These are secondary sources. — [Holafly](https://esim.holafly.com/es/blog/visas/visa-nomada-digital-colombia/); [ColombiaMove guide](https://colombiamove.com/blog/es/colombia-digital-nomad-visa-2026-how-to-apply/)
- **M Trabajador** (art. 74, Res. 5477) is for foreigners with an employment contract with a Colombian legal entity. **M Profesional Independiente** covers independent professionals. — [Affirma Legal](https://www.affirmalegal.com/blog/como-solicitar-la-visa-de-trabajo-en-colombia/)
- There is also a **"fomento a la internacionalización"** visa (V or M), valid **2–3 years**, which counts toward residence. A **V Asistencia Técnica** exists, and the old academic-exchange visa is now **V Estudiante**. No visa specifically for researchers or professors was confirmed. — [Forvis Mazars](https://www.forvismazars.com/co/en/who-we-are/news-publications-and-media/our-news-and-publications/tax-and-legal-news/resolutions-5477-and-5488); [Cancillería table](https://cancilleria.gov.co/sites/default/files/DOCUMENTOS-2026/cuadro-comparativo-de-reclasificacion-de-visas-con-la-resolucion-5477-2022.pdf)
- **Tax:** presence over **183 days** can make a person a Colombian tax resident, so double-tax treaties matter. — [La Nota Económica / guides](https://lanotaeconomica.com.co/movidas-empresarial/nomadas-digitales-que-debe-hacer-para-acceder-a-la-visa-especial-que-ofrece-colombia); [DEV Community 2026](https://dev.to/vyhuynh14/colombia-digital-nomad-visa-requirements-tax-rules-setup-2026-3e5d)
- **Remote-collaboration ease:** UTC-5 with no DST, a full workday of overlap with US Eastern, and 2–3 hours offset from California. — [Alcor](https://alcor.com/your-own-rd-office-news/nearshoring-software-development-to-colombia/)

### Inferences
- **Structuring implication.** A US 501(c)(3) or parent entity paying foreign visiting researchers from abroad keeps them nomad-visa-eligible, with a low threshold and little friction. Once the lab incorporates locally and pays foreigners, it must sponsor M Trabajador visas. Hybrid structures may need legal advice: US-entity fellows plus Colombian-entity local staff.
- Short visiting mentorships (e.g. 1–2 week sprint mentors) may fit visa-exempt short stays, provided no Colombian-source pay is involved. This needs legal confirmation.
- Tighter nomad-visa scrutiny since late 2025 means the lab should provide clear employer letters and expect some rejections.

### Gaps
