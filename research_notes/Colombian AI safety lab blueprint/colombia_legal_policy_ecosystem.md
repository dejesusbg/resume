# Colombian Legal, Regulatory, Policy and Institutional Ecosystem for an AI Research / AI Safety Lab (as of 31 Oct 2026)

Research date: 31 October 2026. Political context: Abelardo de la Espriella is president for 2026-2030 (government that took office in August 2026); MinTIC is Alexandra Falla Zerrate. Everything below is dated. Items marked UNVERIFIED rely on secondary or vendor sources. I could not access some primary PDFs: DNP served CONPES 4144 with SSL/503 errors, and the Cámara returned 503 on the PL 025/2026 full text. Where that happened, the gap is stated.

## 1. Legal entity options, incorporation mechanics, governance (BIC / LTBT analogue), foreign parent and the Delaware flip

### Takeaway
The practical vehicle is a Colombian S.A.S. (Ley 1258 de 2008). It can be incorporated without a notary or minimum capital, it allows multiple-vote share classes, shares held in a *fiducia mercantil*, 10-year transfer locks and binding shareholder agreements. It can also adopt the BIC condition (Ley 1901 de 2018), which is Colombia's public-benefit-corporation analogue. BIC status carries **no special tax regime**. A nonprofit ESAL in the Régimen Tributario Especial (RTE) pays 20% on non-reinvested surplus and can receive donations that are 25% tax-creditable, but DIAN says RTE ESALs **cannot use the CTeI R&D tax discounts/credits**. Foreign (e.g., Delaware) parents can own a Colombian S.A.S. if the investment is registered with Banco de la República. VC/YC pressure still pushes founders toward a US (Delaware), Cayman or Singapore parent.

### Cited Findings
**S.A.S. (Ley 1258 de 2008): governance tools relevant to mission-lock**
- Art. 11: bylaws must state for each class of shares whether voting is single or **multiple**. Art. 12: shares may be held in a **fiducia mercantil**, provided the shareholder registry identifies the trust company and the beneficiaries of the *patrimonio autónomo* and their percentages. Art. 13: bylaws may **prohibit transfer of shares for up to 10 years from issuance**, extendable. Sources conflict on whether extension needs unanimity or a majority, so check the official text. Art. 15: transfers that breach the bylaws are **"ineficaz de pleno derecho"** (void by operation of law). — [Ley 1258 de 2008, Supersociedades PDF](https://www.supersociedades.gov.co/documents/107391/159032/Ley+1258+del+5+de+diciembre+de+2008.pdf/32b063da-d7cc-c55f-2770-1560116b4745); [Accounter summary](https://accounter.co/normatividad/leyes/por-medio-de-la-cual-se-crea-la-sociedad-por-acciones-simplificada)
- Art. 24: SAS shareholder agreements are binding for up to **10 years, renewable for further 10-year periods**. Unlike the S.A. regime, signatories may include shareholders who are also administrators. — [Asuntos Legales, "Acuerdos de accionistas en sociedades SAS"](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/analisis/andres-felipe-quiroga-511176/acuerdos-de-accionistas-en-sociedades-sas-2395746)
- A SAS needs no notary deed and no minimum capital. It is created by private document registered at the Cámara de Comercio. The CCB publishes free model incorporation documents. — [colombiatramita.co guide 2026](https://colombiatramita.co/?p=760); [yo-facturo guide](https://yo-facturo.com/blog/como-constituir-una-sas-en-colombia-paso-a-paso/) (secondary blogs)

**Costs and steps (2026). Secondary sources only; official CCB 2026 fee table not found**
- In 2026 registry fees (*derechos de matrícula/registro*) are computed on the **UVB = COP 12,110**. Estimates for a low-asset SAS at the Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá (CCB) are about COP 171,000 for matrícula. Totals run from about COP 100,000–150,000 for a minimal SAS to COP 500,000–800,000 with advisory help, and blogs quote COP 200,000–1,500,000 in fees depending on declared capital. The departmental/district *impuesto de registro* is quoted at **~0.7% of subscribed capital**, which is UNVERIFIED. — [colombiatramita.co](https://colombiatramita.co/?p=760); [Buk blog](https://www.buk.co/blog/costos-para-crear-una-empresa-en-colombia-guía-completa); [dian.com.co guide 2026](https://dian.com.co/como-constituir-sas-colombia-2026/) (note: dian.com.co is NOT the official DIAN site)
- Typical sequence: draft bylaws, then register at the Cámara de Comercio (which feeds RUES), then obtain the DIAN RUT/NIT, then open a bank account. A foreign shareholder's capital contribution is then registered with Banco de la República (see below). — synthesized from the guides above; Banco de la República step from [Portafolio "Guía Legal de Inversión 2026"](https://www.portafolio.co/files/2026/09/16/RESUMEN-POR-CAPITULOS_Guia-Legal-de-Inversion-2026-1-.docx.pdf)

**Sociedad BIC (Ley 1901 de 18 June 2018; Decreto 2046 de 12 Nov 2019)**
- Any commercial company, existing or future and of any type, may voluntarily adopt the BIC condition. Doing so **does not change the corporate type**, and the company **remains under the ordinary tax regime**. It must add the collective-benefit activities it will pursue to its corporate purpose, and its name must include "BIC" or "Sociedad de Beneficio e Interés Colectivo". Art. 2 lists characteristic activities, mostly labor, diversity, environmental and fair-trade items, and none is AI-specific. Art. 8 says only that the Government "will evaluate" promotion measures. — [Ley 1901 text, ANLA Eureka](https://www.anla.gov.co/wanla/eureka/normativa/leyes/ley-1901-de-2018-sociedades-comerciales-de-beneficio-e-interes-colectivo-bic)
- Adoption is a bylaws reform approved by the majority required for amendments (art. 3). The Superintendencia de Sociedades supervises. The condition is lost voluntarily or by failing the independent standard the company chose to report against. — [Ley 1901, Función Pública](https://www1.funcionpublica.gov.co/eva/gestornormativo/norma.php?i=86982); [Decreto 2046 de 2019, Supersociedades](https://supersociedades.gov.co/documents/guest/Prensa/Noticias/Documentos/2019/Decreto-2046-%20Noviembre%202019.pdf); [Brigard Urrutia on Decreto 2046](https://www.bu.com.co/es/insights/noticias/gobierno-expide-el-decreto-2046-de-2019-el-cual-reglamenta-las-bic)
- Uptake: Asuntos Legales (12 Jul 2026) reports **323 BICs registered since 2018**. This conflicts with MinComercio's 2021 figure of 1,043 BICs and with other claims of about 1,300. The counts probably measure different things (adoptions vs. formal registrations). — [Asuntos Legales](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/consumidor/desde-2018-se-han-registrado-323-sociedades-de-beneficio-e-interes-colectivo-bic-3091344); [La República](https://www.larepublica.co/economia/colombia-el-pais-de-la-region-con-mas-sociedades-de-beneficio-e-interes-colectivo-3266685)

**ESAL (fundación / corporación / asociación) in the Régimen Tributario Especial (RTE)**
- RTE entities pay income tax at a **flat 20% on net benefit/surplus** (art. 356 / 364-5 ET, as amended by Ley 2277 de 2022). The surplus is **exempt if reinvested in the corporate purpose in the following year** (art. 358). Admission and permanence require DIAN qualification and updates under art. 356-2. — [estatuto.co art. 356](https://estatuto.co/356); [Pérez-Llorca, "Obligaciones corporativas ESALes" (Feb 2026)](https://www.perezllorca.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Obligaciones-corporativas-ESALes.pdf)
- Donations to RTE ESALs are not deductible. Instead they give the donor a **25% income-tax discount** (art. 257 ET). That discount plus the art. 256 R&D discount cannot together exceed 25% of tax payable, and any excess carries forward. — [Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/descuento-tributario-por-donaciones-a-entidades-calificadas-en-el-regimen-de-tributacion-especial/); [estatuto.co art. 257](https://estatuto.co/257)
- **DIAN Concepto 153 de 2025**: RTE ESALs and SMEs in the Régimen Simple (RST) **cannot access** the art. 256 R&D discount or the art. 256-1 tax credit. — [INCP, Mar 2025](https://incp.org.co/publicaciones/infoincp-publicaciones/impuestos/2025/03/esal-y-mipymes-en-el-rst-no-pueden-acceder-a-incentivos-tributarios-en-ciencia-tecnologia-e-innovacion/); [Concepto 153 de 2025 PDF](https://incp.org.co/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/Concepto-No.-153-de-2025-–-DIAN.pdf)

**Foreign parent / Delaware flip**
- Foreign direct investment must be **registered with Banco de la República**. Registration gives the right to repatriate capital and remit dividends and sale/liquidation proceeds. Colombia also has a **Compañía Holding Colombiana (CHC)** regime. — [Portafolio, Guía Legal de Inversión 2026 (Sep 2026)](https://www.portafolio.co/files/2026/09/16/RESUMEN-POR-CAPITULOS_Guia-Legal-de-Inversion-2026-1-.docx.pdf); [Asuntos Legales on FDI registration](https://www.asuntoslegales.com.co/consultorio/inversion-extranjera-en-colombia-retos-y-oportunidades-3039161)
- Banco de la República publishes forms for *sustitución* and *cancelación* of foreign investment, which are the mechanics used when shares are swapped into a foreign holding in a flip. — [BanRep guía sustitución](https://d1b4gd4m8561gs.cloudfront.net/sites/default/files/guia-diligenciar-sustitucion-de-inversiones.pdf)
- Sophisticated US investors generally prefer Delaware corporations. A "flip" makes a new Delaware parent that owns the foreign operating company. — [Haynes Boone](https://www.haynesboone.com/news/publications/the-flip-transaction-bringing-your-foreign-startup-into-the-us-investment-market); [Stripe](https://stripe.com/resources/more/what-is-a-delaware-flip); an EAFIT thesis describes the flip as a restructuring of Colombian companies to access venture capital — [EAFIT repository](https://repository.eafit.edu.co/bitstreams/208da222-4c08-4135-8cfd-1e34eb5b6463/download)
- Y Combinator's standard deal terms (updated around January 2026) limited investment to companies incorporated in the **US, Cayman Islands or Singapore**, removing Canada. Canada was re-added after backlash. YC said its top Canadian companies had reincorporated in the US for easier Demo Day capital access. — [BetaKit](https://betakit.com/how-a-small-change-to-y-combinators-terms-sent-waves-through-canadian-tech/); [The Logic](https://thelogic.co/news/exclusive/y-combinator-canada-startups/)

### Inferences
- **Recommended default structure:** a Delaware PBC (or C-corp) parent if US VC/YC money is a goal, owning a Colombian **S.A.S. BIC** that employs staff and runs research locally. FDI is registered with BanRep. Optionally add a separate Colombian **corporación/fundación (RTE ESAL)** for policy, evals and public-interest research, possibly sought for MinCiencias recognition as a Centro de Investigación (Section 4).
- **The ESAL/for-profit split has a tax wrinkle.** An RTE ESAL cannot claim art. 256/256-1 itself (Concepto 153/2025). Taxable companies that co-invest in qualified projects *with* a MinCiencias-recognized actor can claim the 30% discount or 50% credit. A recognized nonprofit research center can therefore be the "recognized actor" partner, and the tax benefit accrues to its corporate funders or to the lab's own SAS. This is an inference from the rules; it needs tax counsel.
- **LTBT analogue:** a Colombian SAS can likely approximate Anthropic's Long-Term Benefit Trust. One design: create a special share class with multiple votes or board-election rights (art. 11). Have it held by a *fiducia mercantil*/patrimonio autónomo (art. 12) whose trust deed obliges a fiduciary to vote according to the safety mission. Lock transfers (art. 13), bind shareholders via art. 24 agreements, and add the BIC purpose clause. Two limits: the 10-year (renewable) caps on transfer restrictions and shareholder agreements, and BIC status, which adds reporting rather than enforceable fiduciary protection. No precedent for an AI-mission trust in Colombia was found.
- If a Delaware PBC parent is used, the trust/LTBT mechanics can sit at the parent under Delaware law, which is better tested. The Colombian SAS BIC then serves mainly local legitimacy and labor and tax purposes.

### Gaps
- Official 2026 CCB fee schedule and the current impuesto de registro rate (Bogotá/Cundinamarca) were not verified from official sources.
- Full text of Ley 1901 arts. 3-6 (administrators' duties to weigh collective interest, *derecho de retiro*, annual report standard) could not be fetched (Función Pública 503). Whether any **BIC-specific tax benefits** exist in 2026 is unconfirmed; the law says ordinary regime.
- Legal maximum term of a *fiducia mercantil* holding shares, and whether a Colombian fiduciaria would accept a mission-voting mandate, were not researched.
- No source explains the BanRep treatment of a flip step by step (sustitución when Colombian holders swap into a Delaware parent).

## 2. National AI policy and regulation (applicable now and coming)

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 Colombia has **no AI statute**. Constitutional Court ruling C-212/2026 confirms "no existe aún una regulación integral de la IA". The binding rules that bite are data protection (Ley 1581/2012 plus SIC Circular Externa 002/2024 on AI) and scattered sectoral or judicial norms. CONPES 4144 (14 Feb 2025) is the national AI policy: six axes, 106 actions to 2030, COP 479.273 billion. It binds only public entities. The government's 2025 AI bill died without a debate on 19 June 2026. New bills were filed in July 2026: **PL 025/2026 Cámara**, which makes MinCiencias the AI authority with EU-style risk tiers and fines up to 3,000 SMMLV, and **PL 065/2026 Senado**. The legislature ends 20 June 2027, and the new government's appetite for regulation appears low.

### Cited Findings
**CONPES 4144 de 2025 (Política Nacional de IA)**
- Approved **14 Feb 2025**. It is a policy, not a law, and obliges only the public entities that own actions: DNP, six ministries and the Presidency. Its **six axes** are (1) ethics and governance; (2) data and infrastructure; (3) R&D&I; (4) capacities and digital talent; (5) risk mitigation; (6) use and adoption in public entities, firms and territories. It has **106 actions to 2030** costing **COP 479,273 million** from the national budget (PGN), with semi-annual monitoring. — [porcontar.com (updated 18 Oct 2026)](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/); [MinTIC press release](https://mintic.gov.co/portal/inicio/Sala-de-prensa/Noticias/399915:Con-aprobacion-de-Conpes-de-IA-Colombia-cuenta-con-Politica-Publica-para-el-desarrollo-de-la-Inteligencia-Artificial)

The figures sum exactly to 479,273. About 59% goes to data/infrastructure, while risk mitigation gets about 1.6% and governance/ethics about 0.1%. — [porcontar.com](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/). **Binding rules in force touching AI (as of Oct 2026)**
- **Ley 1581/2012 + Decreto 1377/2013** require prior informed consent, special care for sensitive data, documented accountability ("responsabilidad demostrada"), and restrictions on **international transfers (art. 26)**. Fines reach **2,000 SMMLV (art. 23)**, and the SIC is the authority. — [porcontar.com](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/)
- **SIC Circular Externa 002 of 21 Aug 2024** ("Lineamientos sobre el tratamiento de datos personales en sistemas de IA") applies to anyone who develops or uses AI with personal data. It requires four filters (suitability, necessity, reasonableness, proportionality), documented risk management, and a **privacy impact assessment before design** when processing is likely high-risk. It also clarifies that data available on the internet is **not "public" merely because it is accessible**. — [SIC Circular PDF](https://sedeelectronica.sic.gov.co/sites/default/files/normativa/Circular%20Externa%20No.%20002%20del%2021%20de%20agosto%20de%202024.pdf); [porcontar.com](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/); [Brigard Urrutia](https://www.bu.com.co/es/insights/noticias/sic-instruye-sobre-el-uso-de-datos-personales-en-sistemas-de-ia)
- The SIC sanctioned **Worldcoin (2025)** for collecting iris data without free consent (via El Colombiano). — [porcontar.com](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/)
- **PL 274/2025 Cámara** (MinCIT + MinCiencias) would update Ley 1581, including provisions on AI processing of personal data. Its status is unknown. — [UNESCO RAM report](https://minciencias.gov.co/sites/default/files/upload/noticias/ram_informe_unesco-colombia_versionfinal_oct2025_2.pdf)
- Other norms: **Ley 2502/2025** adds an AI/deepfake aggravating fine to the crime of personal falsity (in force late July 2026, upheld in C-212/2026). **T-323/2024** covers judges' ChatGPT use (12 principles). **Acuerdo PCSJA24-12243** (Dec 2024) sets rules for AI in the judiciary. **T-067/2025** sets a maximum-disclosure standard for algorithmic transparency. **Directiva Conjunta 007/2025** (Procuraduría/Defensoría) applies to the State. **Auto AC739-2026** (Supreme Court, Feb 2026) fined a lawyer for citing AI-hallucinated cases. **MinTIC's AI security & privacy guidelines (Apr 2026)** are non-binding for the private sector. — [porcontar.com](https://porcontar.com/blog/ley-inteligencia-artificial-colombia/)

### Inferences
- **No AI-specific licensing, registration or model-evaluation duty applies to a research lab in Colombia today.** Compliance centers on Ley 1581 and Circular 002/2024: impact assessments, consent and legal basis for scraped personal data, and cross-border transfer rules when sending data to US clouds.
- **The earliest a horizontal AI law could pass is around June 2027** (PL 025/2026 still needs four debates). The new government's deregulatory tilt and the 2025 bill's failure make passage before the legislature ends uncertain. If PL 025 passes, **MinCiencias** would become the authority, which is the same ministry that runs CTeI funding and actor recognition. That is the natural counterpart for a safety lab offering technical evaluation capacity.
- CONPES 4144 puts about 1.6% of its budget on risk mitigation and nothing visible on frontier-model safety. Safety/evals is therefore a policy white space a private lab could fill, by drafting evaluation guidance or supporting the risk-mitigation axis.

## 3. AI Safety Institute, international networks and openings for a private technical partner

### Takeaway

## 4. Incentives: CTeI tax benefits, MinCiencias recognition, SGR royalties, iNNpulsa, free trade zones, Ley 2069, Medellín (Ruta N / C4IR), Bogotá

### Takeaway
The main levers are: (1) **CNBT-qualified CTeI projects**, giving a 30% income-tax discount (art. 256 ET) or a 50% tax credit (art. 256-1, TIDIS-tradable for SMEs), a **30% discount on PhD salaries**, and a **VAT exemption on imported research equipment for recognized research centers**, which matters for GPU imports; (2) **MinCiencias recognition** as Centro de Investigación or CDT, which takes 90–120 business days and is often a prerequisite; (3) **SGR CTeI royalty calls**, including a COP 630 billion AI-infrastructure call awarded in a contested process to InterNexa. Free trade zones no longer offer a blanket 20% rate. Medellín's Ruta N/C4IR is the most AI-active local ecosystem.

### Cited Findings
**MinCiencias / CNBT tax benefits (MinCiencias page, current 2026)**
- **30% income-tax discount** on amounts invested in CNBT-qualified R&D/innovation projects. MinCiencias says the discount is capped at 30% of tax payable with a 4-year carryforward (art. 258). Alternatively, a **50% tax credit** usable against national taxes, for micro to large firms, where MSMEs with credits over 1,000 UVT can request tradable **TIDIS**. Hiring PhDs (after 25 May 2019) for R&D&I gives a **30% discount on remuneration** (or a 50% credit for MSMEs). CNBT-qualified project resources and researchers' fees count as non-taxable income. **VAT exemption on imports** of equipment for CNBT-qualified projects is available to **MinCiencias-recognized research centers** and recognized education institutions. Donations to the Fondo Francisco José de Caldas are deductible plus a 30% discount. The benefits generally require partnering with a MinCiencias-recognized actor. — [MinCiencias, beneficios tributarios](https://minciencias.gov.co/viceministerios/conocimiento/direccion_transferencia/beneficios-tributarios/cuales-son)
- There is a conflict on the cap. Actualícese says the art. 256 and art. 257 discounts combined cannot exceed **25%** of tax payable. — [Actualícese](https://actualicese.com/descuento-tributario-por-donaciones-a-entidades-calificadas-en-el-regimen-de-tributacion-especial/)

## 5. Physical infrastructure: compute, data centers, cloud, electricity, connectivity

### Takeaway
Colombia has **no frontier-scale public AI compute**. The national supercomputer efforts are small: a BIOS/MinCiencias system targeting 130 TFLOPS. Planned capacity rests on the contested COP 630 billion SGR infrastructure project and a February 2025 G42 MoU with no specifications. **Oracle has the only hyperscale cloud region (Bogotá, Dec 2023)**; AWS, Azure and GCP have no Colombian region as of Sept 2026. Bogotá colocation is growing fast, with roughly 44–58 MW operational and about 300 MW planned. Constraints are **expensive electricity**, at about USD 0.22/kWh industrial (among the highest in LatAm) and gas-import and El Niño risks, plus aging submarine cables landing only on the Caribbean coast. Latency to the US is claimed around 28 ms from the coast.

## 6. Data and IP law for AI research; US chip export controls

### Takeaway
Colombia (Decisión Andina 351 + Ley 23/1982 as amended by Ley 1915/2018) has **no text-and-data-mining exception**. Training on copyrighted works therefore lacks a clear statutory safe harbor. PL 025/2026's ponencia reportedly proposes a TDM exception for scientific research. Only natural persons can be authors, and the DNDA denies protection to purely AI-generated outputs. Personal data in training sets falls under Ley 1581 and SIC Circular 002/2024, under which internet-accessible does not mean public. On US export controls, Colombia was a *de facto* **Tier 2** country under the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule: it is not among the 18 License-Exception-AIA destinations and not arms-embargoed. That rule was de-enforced on 13 May 2025, and as of October 2026 no replacement has been published. Colombia is not in Country Groups D:1/D:4/D:5, so there is no country-based license requirement for advanced GPUs. Ownership by a parent headquartered in China or Macau still triggers licensing.

### Inferences
- **Colombia's Tier 2 status is an inference.** Tier 1 was 18 named, mostly Five Eyes/EU/East Asian allies, with no Latin American country, and Tier 3 was arms-embargoed D:5 countries. Colombia fits neither, so it fell into Tier 2 by default. No source found names Colombia explicitly.
- **Practical export-control posture today:** a Colombian SAS owned by a US/Delaware parent can import or colocate H100/H200/B200-class GPUs without a country-based BIS license, subject to normal end-use and end-user screening. Avoid any China/Macau-headquartered investors in the cap table, which would trigger licensing. Watch for a Diffusion replacement or new certification regime, which could reintroduce Tier-2-style caps or VEU-style data-center requirements.
- **Copyright posture:** with no TDM exception, a lab should prefer licensed, public-domain or openly licensed corpora and document provenance, or train in jurisdictions with TDM/fair-use coverage (US cloud) while doing evals and safety research locally. If PL 025's research TDM exception survives, it would favor a lab recognized as a research actor.
