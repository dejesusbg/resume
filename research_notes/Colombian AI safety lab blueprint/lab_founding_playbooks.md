## Q1. Anthropic: founding, founders, funding, governance, early research. How far was it from "no resources"?

### Takeaway
Anthropic did not start from zero. Seven senior OpenAI people founded it in January 2021, and they were the authors of GPT-3, the scaling laws work and circuits interpretability. Philanthropist and tech-investor backers put in about $704M across the Series A (May 2021) and Series B (April 2022), before any product existed. Claude first shipped in March 2023, about two years after founding. Its credibility on day one came from its founders' publication record and from capital that let it train frontier models. Its safety-branded governance (PBC, LTBT, RSP) came in layers: the PBC from the start, the LTBT and RSP in 2023.

### Cited Findings
**Founding and founders**
- Founded in January 2021 (Wikipedia infobox: January 26, 2021) by seven former OpenAI employees: Dario Amodei, Daniela Amodei, Jared Kaplan, Jack Clark, Chris Olah, Ben Mann, Sam McCandlish and Tom Brown. Dario Amodei had been OpenAI's VP of Research. — [Wikipedia: Anthropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)
- Anthropic's Series A announcement (May 28, 2021) presents its founding credibility directly: "The team has previously published research on GPT-3, circuit-based interpretability, and scaling laws." Dario Amodei is CEO and Daniela Amodei is President. The same post already calls the company a Public Benefit Corporation. — [Anthropic, "Anthropic raises $124 million…" (May 28, 2021)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems)
- Founder publication record, cited from prior knowledge and not re-fetched this session: Tom Brown is first author of the GPT-3 paper, with Ben Mann, Jared Kaplan, Sam McCandlish, Jack Clark and Dario Amodei among its authors — [arXiv:2005.14165](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.14165). Kaplan and McCandlish are first authors of "Scaling Laws for Neural Language Models", with Tom Brown and Dario Amodei among its authors — [arXiv:2001.08361](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.08361).

**Funding rounds**
- **Series A, May 28, 2021: $124M.** Led by Jaan Tallinn (Skype co-founder). Other investors: James McClave, Dustin Moskovitz, the Center for Emerging Risk Research, Eric Schmidt and "others." Stated focus: "steerable, interpretable, and robust" systems, interpretability tools, and human feedback. — [Anthropic (May 28, 2021)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems). Reuters describes it as Anthropic's "first institutional financing." — [Reuters factbox via The Star (Oct 26, 2026)](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/26/factbox-from-amazon-to-google-the-deep-pocketed-investors-in-anthropic). Valuation was about $550M; sources disagree on pre- vs post-money (secondary). — [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/funding-rounds/anthropic-series-a)
- **Series B, about April 2022: about $580M**, led by Sam Bankman-Fried. FTX and Alameda supplied roughly $500M; secondary sources give $500M or $530M. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ftx-to-sell-majority-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million); [The Block](https://www.theblock.co/amp/post/284480/ftx-to-sell-two-thirds-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million-with-buyers-including-jane-street-and-funds-managed-by-fidelity)
- **FTX stake after the bankruptcy:** the FTX estate sold two-thirds of its stake for about $884M (March 2024; buyers included Jane Street, Fidelity and an Abu Dhabi investment firm). It sold the rest for about $452M in mid-2024, about $1.3B in total. — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ftx-to-sell-majority-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million); [Blockworks](https://blockworks.com/news/ftx-to-sell-anthropic-shares); total of about $1.3B per [KuCoin News (secondary)](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/sbf-s-500m-anthropic-bet-from-ftx-collapse-to-380b-valuation)

- **Google:** $500M in October 2023 plus $1.5B committed over time, then $1B more in March 2025 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)). As of October 2026, Reuters reports that Google has committed **up to $40B**, including an initial $10B announced "in April" (year not stated, presumably 2026). — [Reuters via The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/tech/tech-news/2026/10/26/factbox-from-amazon-to-google-the-deep-pocketed-investors-in-anthropic)

**Governance**
- **Public Benefit Corporation:** Anthropic described itself as a PBC already in its May 2021 Series A post. — [Anthropic (May 2021)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems). It is a Delaware PBC. — [Anthropic LTBT post (Sept 19, 2023)](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust)
- **Long-Term Benefit Trust (announced Sept 19, 2023):** an independent body of five "financially disinterested" trustees. It holds a special class of stock, "Class T," created when the charter was amended at the Series C close. That stock lets the Trust elect and remove a growing number of directors, phased in by time and funding milestones, reaching a **board majority within 4 years** of the Series C close. Initial trustees: Jason Matheny (RAND), Kanika Bahl (Evidence Action), Neil Buddy Shah (CHAI, chair), Paul Christiano (ARC) and Zach Robinson (Effective Ventures US). Later additions include Richard Fontaine, Mariano-Florentino Cuéllar and Ben Bernanke. — [Anthropic, "The Long-Term Benefit Trust"](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust)
- **Responsible Scaling Policy (Sept 19, 2023):** defines AI Safety Levels (ASL-1 to ASL-3+), loosely modeled on biosafety levels. Commitments include not deploying ASL-3 models that show meaningful catastrophic misuse risk under red-teaming, defining ASL-4 before reaching ASL-3, and pausing training if safety procedures cannot keep up. RSP changes need board approval after consulting the LTBT. — [Anthropic RSP announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropics-responsible-scaling-policy). METR says its "prototype approach for scaling safeguards to empirical capability measures" has since been adopted by many developers' frontier safety policies. — [METR About](https://metr.org/about)

**Early research outputs and products** (paper links from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session)
- Dec 2021: "A General Language Assistant as a Laboratory for Alignment" — [arXiv:2112.00861](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.00861). "A Mathematical Framework for Transformer Circuits" — [transformer-circuits.pub](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2021/framework/index.html)
- April 2022: "Training a Helpful and Harmless Assistant with RLHF". The HH-RLHF dataset was released publicly. — [arXiv:2204.05862](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05862)
- Aug 2022: "Red Teaming Language Models to Reduce Harms" — [arXiv:2209.07858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.07858)
- Dec 2022: "Constitutional AI: Harmlessness from AI Feedback" — [arXiv:2212.08073](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.08073). Constitutional AI trains a model to follow a written set of principles. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)
- Claude's first version finished training in summer 2022 but was held back for safety testing. It was released to approved users in March 2023. Claude 2 became public in July 2023. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic)
- April 2025: Anthropic's $1M check into Goodfire's Series A was reportedly its first-ever startup investment. — [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/?p=2692562); [SiliconANGLE (Apr 18, 2025)](https://siliconangle.com/?p=693463)

### Inferences
- **Size of the gap:** Anthropic raised about $704M in its first ~15 months ($124M + ~$580M). That is about 4,800 times Timaeus's initial $145k budget, about 460 times Apollo's $1.54M Open Philanthropy startup grant, and infinitely more than EleutherAI's $0 cash start (see Q2). In addition, three founders had co-authored GPT-3 and two led the scaling-laws paper. A Colombian founder starting from zero cannot copy Anthropic's entry point (a frontier model lab funded at the outset). They can copy its research and governance ideas.
- Anthropic's earliest money came from the same philanthropic network that later funded the small safety orgs: Tallinn, Moskovitz (whose Good Ventures funds Open Philanthropy) and the Center for Emerging Risk Research. Its first outside institutional money was mission-aligned rather than standard VC.
- The governance pieces are cheap to copy and need no capital: a PBC charter, mission-protected board seats (Apollo copied this in 2026), a published RSP-style scaling and deployment policy, and openly released datasets like HH-RLHF. Building them in early is how a small org can signal "Anthropic-like" credibility.
- Anthropic waited about two years and spent hundreds of millions before its first product. A zero-resource lab cannot afford that delay; it needs revenue or grant income within months.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable source on Anthropic's headcount at founding or its compute spend in 2021–2022.
- Moskovitz's role in the Series A (lead or participant) and whether the $550M valuation was pre- or post-money are inconsistent across secondary sources.
- The exact share of the Series B that came from FTX/Alameda ($500M vs $530M) differs across secondary sources.
- I did not verify the RSP's later versions (2024–2026 revisions) in this session.

## Q2. How smaller and bootstrapped AI safety orgs started: founders, money, team, first output, business model

### Takeaway
Nearly every small safety org started with (1) founders who had a credential or network tie (PhD lab, MATS mentor, prior org), (2) a first check of $100k–$2M from a small number of EA-aligned funders (Open Philanthropy, now renamed Coefficient Giving; Manifund regrantors; the Survival and Flourishing Fund; FTX Future Fund before November 2022) or donated compute, and (3) one public, citable output within 6–12 months. For-profit safety companies (Gray Swan, Haize, Goodfire, Apollo's 2026 PBC) appear once there is paying demand from frontier labs and enterprises for red-teaming, evals or interpretability tooling.

### Cited Findings

**EleutherAI: a volunteer Discord collective that grew into a nonprofit institute (the purest "zero resources" case)**
- Started around July 7, 2020 as the "LibreAI" Discord server, soon renamed EleutherAI. It grew out of Shawn Presser's Discord, where Connor Leahy proposed replicating GPT-3. Co-founders: Connor Leahy, Sid Black and Leo Gao. — [EleutherAI one-year retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/); [Wikipedia: Connor Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy)
- **Compute was donated, not bought.** Early work ran on TPUs from Google's TPU Research Cloud, which Leahy had access to from earlier GPT-2 work. In January 2021 CoreWeave agreed to provide GPUs for a GPT-3-scale model, and "no money was exchanged." — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/)
- Outputs: The Pile (800GB dataset, preprint January 1, 2021), GPT-Neo 1.3B/2.7B (March 21, 2021), GPT-J-6B (June 2021; the largest open GPT-3-style model at the time) and GPT-NeoX-20B (February 2022). — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/); [Wikipedia: Connor Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy). Pile paper: [arXiv:2101.00027](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.00027) (link from prior knowledge).
- Lessons the team itself recorded: TensorFlow and TPU tooling was a major obstacle (they switched to JAX), web data cleaning was much harder than expected, codebase problems delayed the Neo release by months, and a GPU shortage hurt scaling. — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/)
- **Becoming an institution (March 2023):** incorporated as the EleutherAI Institute, a nonprofit run by Stella Biderman, Curtis Huebner and Shivanshu Purohit. It was funded by donations and grants from Stability AI, Hugging Face, Nat Friedman, Lambda Labs and Canva, and the new structure supported more than 20 full-time staff. Its focus moved from training large models toward interpretability, alignment and evaluation. — [TechCrunch (Mar 2, 2023)](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/02/stability-ai-hugging-face-and-canva-back-new-ai-research-nonprofit). 2024 operating budget of about $2.3M, per an aggregator (secondary). — [Impala profile](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/92-2215190/overview)

**Redwood Research: a grant-funded nonprofit that created the "AI control" agenda**
- 501(c)(3) in Berkeley founded in 2021 (June 2021 per a secondary wiki) by Buck Shlegeris and Nate Thomas. Shlegeris later became CEO and Ryan Greenblatt Chief Scientist (secondary). — [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/organizations/redwood-research)
- Open Philanthropy funding: four grants totaling $9.42M (Nov 2021), $10.7M over 18 months (Aug 2022) and $5.3M (June 2023), per grant-database aggregators (secondary). — [Open Philanthropy grant records via search](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/?p=15567); [LongtermWiki grant record](https://www.longtermwiki.com/sourcing/grant/zlYR-k8r4n)
- Outputs: "AI Control: Improving Safety Despite Intentional Subversion" (ICML 2024 oral) and "Alignment Faking in LLMs" with Anthropic. It advises Google DeepMind and Anthropic, partnered with UK AISI on an AI-control safety case, and published a joint incident report with METR in August 2026. — [Redwood Research homepage](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/). Early project, "Adversarial Training for High-Stakes Reliability" (2022): [arXiv:2205.01663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.01663) (link from prior knowledge).

**METR (formerly ARC Evals): an evals nonprofit that refuses lab money**
- Incubated in 2022 inside Paul Christiano's Alignment Research Center under Beth Barnes. Spun out in December 2023 as an independent 501(c)(3) named METR (secondary). — [LongtermWiki / Wikipedia mirrors](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/METR)
- As of 2026: Beth Barnes is Founder and CEO, and the staff is about 45 (4 leadership, 31 technical, 7 operations, 3 policy). Funding comes from foundations, individuals, pooled funds and the UK AI Security Institute, plus a small technical-assistance contract with the EU AI Office. METR "has not accepted funding from AI companies" but **does accept free tokens from AI companies** for evals. It cannot accept donations from frontier-lab employees. — [METR About](https://metr.org/about)
- A $17M Audacious Project grant is reported by a secondary wiki only. — [LongtermWiki](https://longterm-wiki.vercel.app/organizations/metr)

**Apollo Research: an evals nonprofit (2023) that became a PBC with a product arm (2026)**
- Announced May 30, 2023 in London by Marius Hobbhahn. It had "starter funding" but faced a $1.4M first-year gap; it estimated $7–10M as the "total useful" amount and tied hiring to closing the gap. — [Announcing Apollo Research (LessWrong mirror)](https://forum.nunosempere.com/posts/ysC6crBKhDBGZfob3/announcing-apollo-research). Co-founders reportedly included Lee Sharkey, with a founding team of about 8 (secondary). — [ea-crux wiki](https://ea-crux-project.vercel.app/knowledge-base/organizations/safety-orgs/apollo-research)
- Open Philanthropy recommended a **$1,535,480 startup grant** (June 2023). — [Open Philanthropy: Apollo Research startup funding](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/apollo-research-startup-funding). Manifund gave $310k, its largest grant at the time, coordinated across several regrantors. — [Manifund "What we're funding weeks 2-4"](https://manifund.substack.com/p/what-were-funding-weeks-2-4)
- Hobbhahn's one-year update (May 2024) said fundraising took more time and effort than expected. At 18 months (Dec 2024), Apollo still had "room for funding." — [search summary of May 2024 update](https://manifund-keep7wiqa-mantic.vercel.app/mariushobbhahn); [Apollo 18-Month Update](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-18-month-update/)
- **PBC conversion (Jan 20, 2026):** Apollo spun off from its fiscal sponsor into a Public Benefit Corporation. Reason given: demand for frontier-AI safety research and evals is growing faster than a nonprofit can serve. It raised an oversubscribed seed round led by 50Y (with Juniper Ventures, Macroscopic Ventures, SAIF and others; amount undisclosed). The 3rd and 6th board seats are "mission seats" held by directors independent of the company and its funders; the first is Daniel Kokotajlo. A product arm is building "Watcher," observability and control for AI coding agents. An external firm valued the IP and the PBC bought it from the nonprofit at that value. Some research remains philanthropically funded. — [Apollo Research, "Apollo Research is becoming a PBC"](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-research-is-becoming-a-pbc/)

**FAR.AI: a research nonprofit founded by a fresh PhD**
- Founded in Q3 2022 and incorporated in October 2022 by Adam Gleave (UC Berkeley PhD under Stuart Russell) and Karl Berzins. — [FAR.AI About](https://www.far.ai/about); [Gleave profile, SCAI 2025](https://www.scai.gov.sg/2025/participants-of-scai-2025/adam-gleave/)
- Notable early output: "Adversarial Policies Beat Superhuman Go AIs" (Nov 2022), which exploited KataGo — [arXiv:2211.00241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00241) (link from prior knowledge). FAR also runs events, including the venue where Buck Shlegeris presented AI control. — [FAR.AI event page](https://www.far.ai/events/sessions/buck-shlegeris-ai-control-and-why-ai-security-people-should-care)

**Timaeus: the smallest documented start ($145k)**
- Announced in late October 2023 to pursue "developmental interpretability" (singular learning theory), led by Jesse Hoogland with Daniel Murfet. **Its first research funding was a Manifund regrant of about $142–145k** (regrantors included Evan Hubinger and Ryan Kidd). Hoogland said this let them start "months earlier." It is fiscally sponsored by Ashgro. Later funding: about $500k from SFF, then about $2M from Open Phil and SFF. By its 2024 review it had about $2.5M for 2025 and a team of 8. — [Manifund regrant review](https://manifund.substack.com/p/reviewing-our-ai-safety-regrants); [Timaeus in 2024](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/gGAXSfQaiGBCwBJH5/timaeus-in-2024); [Announcing Timaeus](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/aaa9pEwnvyeE2TYvg/announcing-timaeus)

**Epoch AI: forecasting and data, grant-funded and concentrated on one funder**
- Founded in April 2022. Open Philanthropy gave a $1.96M general-support grant. A Manifund page says about 75% of its funding came from Open Philanthropy. — [Epoch Impact Report 2022](https://epoch.ai/blog/epoch-impact-report-2022); [Epoch "Our Funding"](https://epoch.ai/our-funding)

**Palisade Research: demonstrations of dangerous capabilities for policymakers**
- Founded around 2023 (inferred from tax filings and copyright dates). Open Philanthropy grants of $2,123,463 for general support (2025). 2024 operating budget of about $1.5M (secondary). — [Impala profile](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/93-1591014/overview); [LongtermWiki grant records](https://www.longtermwiki.com/grants/eNUYEA1UNp)

**Transluce: a philanthropy-funded nonprofit for open interpretability tools**
- Co-founded by UC Berkeley professor Jacob Steinhardt (Founder and CEO) and Sarah Schwettmann (co-founder and Chief Scientist). It is a San Francisco nonprofit funded by philanthropy "so that the technology can be designed openly and in the public interest." A Manifund page sought $2M and had raised $40k by a January 23, 2026 deadline. — [UC Berkeley Statistics](https://statistics.berkeley.edu/node/8673); [Manifund Transluce project](https://manifund.org/projects/transluce-fund-scalable-democratic-oversight-of-ai); [Introducing Transluce](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/BFamsq52ctyRziDgE/introducing-transluce-a-letter-from-the-founders)

**Simplex: theory-driven interpretability with small models**
- Co-founded by Paul Riechers (theoretical physics PhD, UC Davis) and Adam Shai (computational neuroscience). It studies the geometry of internal representations. Key paper: "Transformers Represent Belief State Geometry in their Residual Stream" (May 2024), which used small transformers. A 2026 preprint applies the method to Gemma-2-9B. Both founders are MATS mentors, and Simplex is linked to the Astera Institute (job listings are posted under Astera). — [MATS stream page](https://www.matsprogram.org/stream/shai); [arXiv:2405.15943](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15943v1); [Astera Institute post](https://asterainstitute.substack.com/p/understanding-intelligence-simplex); [Astera job listing](https://startup.jobs/research-scientist-simplex-astera-institute-8138804)

**Conjecture: a VC-funded alignment startup that pivoted, then wound down**
- Founded in March 2022 (some sources say late 2021) by Connor Leahy, Sid Black and Gabriel Alfour, all from EleutherAI. Funding was reported at about $10M from VCs (2022 critique), and $25M+ by December 2022 per a secondary wiki. — [Wikipedia: Connor Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy); [EA Forum critique of Conjecture](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/gkfMLX4NWZdmpikto/critiques-of-prominent-ai-safety-labs-conjecture); [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/organizations/conjecture)
- Pivoted in February 2023 to "Cognitive Emulation," building bounded, interpretable systems rather than aligning LLMs. A Dec 2024 roadmap described "cognitive software" (secondary). — [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E70)
- **March 2026:** Leahy announced in a retrospective that "the Conjecture project was drawing to a close." He then became US Director of ControlAI, an advocacy nonprofit. — [Wikipedia: Connor Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy)

**Gray Swan AI: an academic red-teaming group spun into a for-profit AI security company**
- Spun out of Carnegie Mellon's AI safety research. Founders: Matt Fredrikson (CEO) and Zico Kolter (Chief Scientist). Forbes reported a **$5.5M seed** from an undisclosed nontraditional investor plus friends and family, and said the trio founded it "last September" (about Sept 2023). — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/?p=112677)
- **$40M Series A (May 28, 2026)**, co-led by Wing VC and Madrona. It has more than 20 customers across frontier labs and enterprises, and Anthropic, OpenAI and Meta cite it in system cards. Products: Shade (automated red-teaming), Cygnal (runtime monitoring) and **Arena, a crowdsourced red-teaming network of more than 15,000 researchers**. — [Gray Swan Series A announcement](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/gray-swan-announces-series-a)
- Fredrikson and Kolter co-authored the GCG universal jailbreak paper (July 2023), which preceded the company — [arXiv:2307.15043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043) (link from prior knowledge).

**Haize Labs: an automated red-teaming startup founded by new graduates**
- Founded by three Harvard graduates (Leonard Tang among them) in New York in 2023 or 2024 (sources conflict). Raised about $1M from angels, then a **$12.5M seed led by General Catalyst at a $100M post-money valuation**, about seven months after founding. Total funding is listed as $25.5M by Caplight. All figures are secondary and inconsistent. It reportedly counts OpenAI and Anthropic as customers. — [PitchBook News](https://www.pitchbook.com/news/articles/general-catalyst-haize-labs-100-million-valuation); [Signalbase](https://www.trysignalbase.com/news/funding/haize-labs-closes-125m-seed-round-for-ai-trust-safety); [Caplight](https://www.caplight.com/company/haizelabs)

**Goodfire: a venture-funded interpretability company**
- Seed of about $7M (Aug 2024, aggregator). **Series A of $50M (Apr 17, 2025)** led by Menlo Ventures, with Lightspeed, Anthropic ($1M, its first startup investment), B Capital, Work-Bench, Wing and South Park Commons; reported valuation $200M. Product: the "Ember" interpretability platform. — [SiliconANGLE (Apr 18, 2025)](https://siliconangle.com/?p=693463); [Pillsbury press release](https://pillsburylaw.com/en/news-and-insights/pillsbury-goodfire-ai-50m-series-a-funding-round-launch-platform-ember.html). A **Series B of $150M at $1.25B (Feb 2026)**, led by B Capital, rests on one secondary source; verify before use. — [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/goodfire)

**Cohere For AI (now Cohere Labs) Aya: a distributed open-science collaboration (the best model for Colombia)**
- Launched in January 2023 by Cohere's nonprofit research lab. It drew **more than 3,000 independent researchers in 119 countries**. The Aya model (Feb 2024) covers 101 languages, more than 50 of them previously underserved. The instruction dataset has 513M data points in 114 languages. Released under Apache 2.0. Follow-ups: Aya 23 and Aya Vision (8B and 32B). Performance claims come from Cohere itself. — [Axios (Feb 13, 2024)](https://www.axios.com/2024/02/13/open-source-ai-languages); [SiliconANGLE (Feb 13, 2024)](https://siliconangle.com/2024/02/13/cohere-ai-unveils-aya-multilingual-open-source-ai-101-languages/); [Cohere research page](https://Cohere.ai/research). Aya paper: [arXiv:2402.07827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.07827) (link from prior knowledge).

**The pipeline: MATS**
- By May 2024 MATS had supported 213 scholars and 47 mentors. Its Summer 2025 cohort had 98 scholars and 57 mentors (secondary). In a 2024 survey of the first four cohorts, 78% of respondents worked on alignment or control or did independent alignment research, and 68% had published alignment research. Alumni founded Apollo Research among others (secondary). — [LongtermWiki: MATS](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/organizations/safety-orgs/mats); [80,000 Hours job board](https://jobs.80000hours.org/organisations/ml-alignment-theory-scholars)

### Inferences
- **Founder credentials matter as much as money.** Every org above was founded by someone with a recognizable credential: a Berkeley or CMU PhD or professorship (FAR, Transluce, Gray Swan), a MATS mentor role or alignment-community track record (Apollo, Simplex, Timaeus), or open-source fame (EleutherAI, then Conjecture). Haize Labs (new Harvard graduates) is the main exception, and it raised on product traction rather than research credibility.
- **First checks cluster at $100k–$2M and come from a handful of EA-network funders:** Open Phil/Coefficient Giving, Manifund regrantors, SFF and previously FTX Future Fund. Evidence: Timaeus $145k; Apollo $1.54M from Open Phil plus $310k from Manifund; Epoch $1.96M; Palisade about $2.1M; Redwood $9.42M in year one, the outlier because its founders were already known to Open Phil.
- **EleutherAI is the only well-documented case that started with literally $0**, using donated TPU Research Cloud and CoreWeave compute plus Discord volunteers. It took about 2.7 years (July 2020 to March 2023) to become a funded institute with 20+ staff.
- **"Nonprofit first, PBC later" is now an established pattern** (Apollo 2026). Apollo told funders in 2023 that a conversion might happen, which protected those relationships, and the PBC bought the nonprofit's IP at an independent valuation.
- For a Colombian founder, Aya plus EleutherAI is the most transferable combination: a distributed volunteer collaboration on an underserved-language problem, using donated compute and open releases.

### Gaps
- I could not verify primary sources for Goodfire's founders (believed to be Eric Ho, Dan Balsam and Tom McGrath, ex-Google DeepMind), Palisade's founder (believed to be Jeffrey Ladish), Epoch's founder (believed to be Jaime Sevilla) or Transluce's launch date (believed to be Oct 2024). Treat these as unverified.
- I found no reliable starting team sizes for Redwood, METR, FAR.AI, Epoch or Palisade, and no amount for Apollo's 2026 seed.
- METR's and Redwood's first notable outputs (for example, METR's pre-deployment GPT-4 evaluation in 2023) were not re-confirmed this session.
- I found no primary figure on Haize Labs' founding year or total funding (sources conflict).

## Q3. A realistic staged path: collective, then nonprofit, then for-profit evals/safety company, then (maybe) model developer

### Takeaway
The documented path has four rungs with roughly order-of-magnitude jumps in money. Stage 0 (volunteer collective) costs $0 cash with donated compute. Stage 1 (seed-funded research team) costs $100k–$500k per year. Stage 2 (a credible nonprofit research or evals org) costs $1.5M–$10M per year. Stage 3 (a for-profit or PBC safety and security vendor) raises $5M–$50M. Stage 4 (frontier model developer) needed about $700M before a first product in Anthropic's case. No org in this sample has climbed from Stage 0 to Stage 4. Anthropic started at Stage 4, and EleutherAI-to-Conjecture shows that a jump to venture funding without a product can fail.

### Cited Findings
- **Stage 0, collective ($0 cash):** EleutherAI's Discord volunteers used donated TPU Research Cloud and CoreWeave compute ("no money was exchanged") and shipped The Pile, GPT-Neo and GPT-J within about 12 months. — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/). Aya assembled 3,000+ researchers in 119 countries around one open project. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2024/02/13/open-source-ai-languages)
- **Stage 1, small funded team ($100k–$500k per year, 2–5 people):** Timaeus started on about $145k (late 2023) and reached a team of 8 and about $2.5M for 2025 within about 14 months. — [Timaeus in 2024](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/gGAXSfQaiGBCwBJH5/timaeus-in-2024). Individual on-ramps: Anthropic Fellows pays $3,850 per week for about 4 months, plus about $15k per month in compute (2026 listings; the original 2024 pilot was about $10k per month). — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/anthropic-fellows-program); [Introducing the Anthropic Fellows Program](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/xwebc6CLWuLmahcx6/introducing-the-anthropic-fellows-program). MATS places alumni into labs or new orgs. — [LongtermWiki: MATS](https://www.longtermwiki.com/knowledge-base/organizations/safety-orgs/mats)
- **Stage 2, nonprofit research or evals org ($1.5M–$10M per year, 8–45 people):** Apollo started with $1.54M from Open Phil plus $310k from Manifund and estimated $7–10M as useful; fundraising "took more time and effort than expected." — [Open Philanthropy](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/grants/apollo-research-startup-funding); [Manifund](https://manifund.substack.com/p/what-were-funding-weeks-2-4). Redwood received $9.42M in 2021. — [Open Philanthropy record](https://www.openphilanthropy.org/?p=15567). METR has about 45 staff, funded by donations and AISI, with a small EU AI Office contract and free lab tokens. — [METR About](https://metr.org/about). EleutherAI Institute has 20+ full-time staff and about $2.3M in 2024 (secondary). — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/02/stability-ai-hugging-face-and-canva-back-new-ai-research-nonprofit); [Impala](https://impala.digital/public/profiles/92-2215190/overview)
- **Government money as a Stage 2 source:** the UK AISI-led Alignment Project (launched July 2025) is £15M with typical grants of £50k–£1M and up to £5M in AWS compute credits. Partners include the Canadian AISI, Schmidt Sciences, AWS and Anthropic. Applications appeared closed as of the latest listing. — [UK Find a Grant](https://find-government-grants.service.gov.uk/grants/the-alignment-project-1); [Transformer](https://www.transformernews.ai/p/uk-launches-15-million-ai-alignment)
- **Stage 3, for-profit or PBC safety vendor ($5M–$50M raised):** Gray Swan went from academic paper to a $5.5M seed (about 2023) to a $40M Series A (May 2026) with 20+ customers. — [Forbes Australia](https://www.forbes.com.au/?p=112677); [Gray Swan](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/gray-swan-announces-series-a). Haize Labs raised a $12.5M seed at $100M about 7 months after founding (secondary). — [PitchBook](https://www.pitchbook.com/news/articles/general-catalyst-haize-labs-100-million-valuation). Goodfire went from a $7M seed to a $50M Series A. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/?p=693463). Apollo converted from nonprofit to PBC with a product arm (Jan 2026). — [Apollo](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-research-is-becoming-a-pbc/)
- **Stage 4, model developer:** Anthropic raised $124M (May 2021) and about $580M (2022) before its first product (Claude, March 2023). — [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-124-million-to-build-more-reliable-general-ai-systems); [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ftx-to-sell-majority-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic). EleutherAI did train models at Stage 0 (GPT-J-6B, NeoX-20B), but only with donated compute, at sub-frontier scale, and later moved away from model training toward interpretability and evals. — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/03/02/stability-ai-hugging-face-and-canva-back-new-ai-research-nonprofit)

### Inferences
- The realistic "Anthropic-like" target for a zero-resource Colombian founder over 3–5 years is Stage 2 or Stage 3: a safety research nonprofit or PBC that publishes, sells evals and red-teaming, and ideally adopts Anthropic-style governance (PBC charter, mission seats, a published safety policy). "Model developer" should mean fine-tuning or post-training open-weight models (for example for Spanish or indigenous languages), not frontier pretraining.
- Revenue for Stage 3 in this sample comes from three sources: (a) red-teaming and evals contracts with frontier labs, who cite vendors in system cards (Gray Swan, Haize); (b) runtime monitoring or observability products (Gray Swan Cygnal, Apollo Watcher); (c) interpretability platforms (Goodfire Ember). Crowdsourced red-teaming (Gray Swan Arena, 15,000+ participants) is a model that could use a low-cost Latin American talent pool.
- Per-stage timing in this sample: EleutherAI went from Stage 0 to Stage 2 in about 2.7 years. Timaeus went from Stage 1 to a small Stage 2 in about 14 months. Apollo went from Stage 2 to Stage 3 in about 2.6 years (May 2023 to Jan 2026). Gray Swan went from academic lab to Series A in about 2.7 years.

### Gaps
- I found no detailed per-stage compute budgets, other than the Anthropic Fellows figure (about $10–15k per fellow per month) and the AWS £5M pool in the Alignment Project.
- I found no case of a Latin American or Global South safety org moving through these stages; this sample is entirely US, UK, EU and Australia-based (Timaeus has Australian roots via Murfet).

## Q4. Safety research agendas that are realistically doable with low compute, open-weight models or API access in 2026

### Takeaway
Many of the most-cited safety results of 2023–2025 came from teams of 4–8 people using open-weight models (up to about 72B) or public APIs (including public fine-tuning APIs), not from frontier-scale training. Agendas that are realistic on a small budget in 2026: mechanistic interpretability with released SAEs and transcoders (Gemma Scope 2), representation-level analysis such as refusal directions, automated jailbreaking and red-teaming, AI control protocols built on API models, model organisms built by fine-tuning (emergent misalignment), evals and benchmarks, and multilingual safety and data work (Aya-style).

### Cited Findings
- **Mechanistic interpretability on open models:** Google DeepMind's **Gemma Scope 2** releases SAEs and transcoders for every layer of Gemma 3 at 270M, 1B, 4B, 12B and 27B (pretrained and instruction-tuned), plus crosscoders and cross-layer transcoders for 270M and 1B. It loads through SAELens and can be browsed on Neuronpedia. — [Gemma Scope page](https://deepmind.google/models/gemma/gemma-scope/); [HF: gemma-scope-2-12b-it](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-scope-2-12b-it). The original Gemma Scope paper: [arXiv:2408.05147](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.05147)
- **Representation-level safety on open models (7 authors, a Neel Nanda–mentored team):** "Refusal in Language Models Is Mediated by a Single Direction" (NeurIPS 2024; arXiv June 2024) showed across **13 open chat models up to 72B** that refusal is governed by one direction. They turned this into a rank-one weight edit that jailbreaks models more simply than fine-tuning does. Code is public. — [arXiv:2406.11717](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.11717); [NeurIPS proceedings](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/f545448535dfde4f9786555403ab7c49-Abstract.html)
- **Model organisms of misalignment, made cheaply:** "Emergent Misalignment" (ICML 2025; 8 authors from Truthful AI, UCL, CLR, Warsaw UT, Toronto, UK AISI and UC Berkeley) fine-tuned GPT-4o on about 6,000 insecure-code examples and got broad misalignment on unrelated prompts. The effect also appeared in open Qwen2.5-Coder-32B. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/betley25a.html); [The Register via ciso2ciso](https://ciso2ciso.com/?p=162144); [CLR mirror](https://longtermrisk.org/research/emergent-misalignment-narrow-finetuning-can-produce-broadly-misaligned/); [Quanta (Aug 13, 2025)](https://www.quantamagazine.org/the-ai-was-fed-sloppy-code-it-turned-into-something-evil-20250813/)
- **AI control, which can be run with API models:** Redwood's "AI Control" (ICML 2024 oral) created the agenda. Its protocols for monitoring untrusted models have since been built on by labs. — [Redwood Research](https://www.redwoodresearch.org/); paper [arXiv:2312.06942](https://arxiv.org/abs/2312.06942) (4 authors; link from prior knowledge).
- **Theory-first interpretability on toy models:** Simplex's belief-state-geometry work used small transformers on synthetic data, and the method was later applied to Gemma-2-9B (2026 preprint). — [arXiv:2405.15943](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2405.15943v1); [Astera post](https://asterainstitute.substack.com/p/understanding-intelligence-simplex). Timaeus's developmental interpretability started on a $145k budget. — [Manifund](https://manifund.substack.com/p/reviewing-our-ai-safety-regrants)
- **Red-teaming and jailbreak research:** the GCG automated adversarial-suffix attack (CMU and collaborators, 2023) was developed on open models and later became the research base for Gray Swan. Gray Swan's Arena now crowdsources red-teaming from more than 15,000 people. — [arXiv:2307.15043](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.15043) (link from prior knowledge); [Gray Swan](https://www.grayswan.ai/news/gray-swan-announces-series-a)
- **Adversarial robustness of "superhuman" systems on modest compute:** FAR.AI's adversarial policies against KataGo (2022). — [arXiv:2211.00241](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.00241) (link from prior knowledge)
- **Evals funded by free tokens:** METR runs capability and risk evals on free tokens donated by AI companies while refusing their cash. — [METR About](https://metr.org/about)
- **Multilingual safety and data:** Aya's 513M-example, 114-language instruction dataset and open models were built mostly by distributed volunteers. — [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/02/13/cohere-ai-unveils-aya-multilingual-open-source-ai-101-languages/)
- **Open research substrates:** EleutherAI released The Pile and GPT-J, and later moved to interpretability and evals. — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/). Its Pythia suite of open models with training checkpoints is widely used for low-compute interpretability. — [arXiv:2304.01373](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.01373) (link from prior knowledge). Early SAE results came from a small independent team that included Lee Sharkey, later an Apollo co-founder. — [arXiv:2309.08600](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.08600) (link from prior knowledge).

**Researcher access programs and subsidies (as of 2026)**
- **Anthropic External Researcher Access Program:** free API credits for "AI safety and alignment topics we consider high priority." Covers standard models only (no non-public models, no fine-tuning API) and does not apply to Claude.ai. Groups apply once and receive credits to one org ID. The German help page states $1,000 as the standard grant, with more in rare cases. The English page is dated March 16, 2026. — [Anthropic support: ERAP](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/9125743-what-is-the-external-researcher-access-program); [German version](https://support.anthropic.com/de/articles/9125743-was-ist-das-external-researcher-access-program)
- **Anthropic AI for Science:** API credits for academic and nonprofit science, focused on biology. A third-party report says up to $20k over 6 months. — [Anthropic support](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11199177-anthropic-s-ai-for-science-program); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/anthropic-unveils-ai-for-science-program)
- **Anthropic Fellows Program (2026):** $3,850 per week, about $15k per month compute, about 4 months, with tracks in AI Safety, Security, ML Systems, RL and Economics/Policy. Cohorts started in May and July 2026. — [Built In](https://builtin.com/articles/anthropic-fellows-program); [Granted AI](https://grantedai.com/news/anthropic-ai-safety-fellows-2026-compute-stipend)
- **OpenAI Researcher Access Program:** up to $1,000 in API credits valid 12 months, tied to the applicant's institution. Applications reopened on Feb 3, 2025; reviews are reportedly quarterly. — [OpenAI form](https://openai.com/form/researcher-access-program); [OpenAI FAQ](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/10139500-researcher-access-program-faq); [OpenAI grants page](https://grants.openai.com/prog/openai_researcher_access_program/)
- **Google TPU Research Cloud:** the source of EleutherAI's early compute. — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/)
- **UK AISI Alignment Project:** £15M, grants of £50k–£1M, up to £5M in AWS credits (July 2025). — [UK Find a Grant](https://find-government-grants.service.gov.uk/grants/the-alignment-project-1)

### Inferences
- The cheapest high-impact niches for a Colombian team: (1) **Spanish and Latin American language safety evals and red-teaming**, testing whether refusal and jailbreak defenses generalize across languages (refusal-direction methods on open models plus Aya-style volunteer data); (2) **interpretability on Gemma 3 with Gemma Scope 2**, which needs only a single GPU for small models; (3) **model-organism studies** through public fine-tuning APIs or open 7–32B models; (4) **AI control protocols** run on cheap API models. All four have well-cited small-team precedents.
- Free credits from the access programs are small ($1,000 each from Anthropic and OpenAI). They help bootstrap a first paper but cannot sustain an org. The meaningful compute subsidies are the Anthropic Fellows budget (about $15k per month per fellow), AWS credits through the Alignment Project, and in-kind donations like EleutherAI's TRC and CoreWeave deals. METR's model (free tokens from labs, cash from philanthropy and governments) is a working template for an independent evals org.
- Anthropic's ERAP excludes fine-tuning, so model-organism work in the Emergent Misalignment style needs OpenAI's fine-tuning API or open-weight models.

### Gaps
- Several arXiv IDs (2312.06942, 2307.15043, 2211.00241, 2304.01373, 2309.08600, 2402.07827, 2205.01663 and the Anthropic papers in Q1) are cited from prior knowledge, not re-fetched this session. Author counts and model details for those papers should be spot-checked.
- I did not confirm the 2026 status of NDIF/NNsight (remote access to large open-model internals for academics) or Google DeepMind and Meta researcher programs.
- I did not find whether Anthropic's or OpenAI's programs restrict applicants by country, which matters for Colombia.
- I found no compute-cost figures (GPU-hours) for the small-team papers above.

## Q5. Common failure modes of small safety orgs

### Takeaway
The main documented failure modes are: (1) **funder concentration and sudden funder collapse** (FTX Future Fund, November 2022); (2) **slower-than-planned fundraising** even for well-connected teams (Apollo); (3) **host-institution friction** for groups embedded in universities (FHI's 2024 closure); (4) **strategy churn after taking venture money without a product**, ending in wind-down (Conjecture: founded 2022, pivoted 2023, closing in 2026); (5) **crowdfunding that falls far short** (Transluce's Manifund page).

### Cited Findings
- **FTX Future Fund collapse:** the Future Fund team (Nick Beckstead, Leopold Aschenbrenner, Avital Balwit, Ketan Ramakrishnan and Will MacAskill) resigned on November 10, 2022 and said many committed grants would likely go unpaid. Reported size: about $132M distributed across 262 grants, or about $160M committed (sources differ). FTX's bankruptcy date was November 11, 2022, with a 2-year window for clawback claims. As of April 2023 no Future Fund grantee had received a formal demand letter. Open Phil invited affected grantees in AI risk, biosecurity and longtermist community-building to apply for replacement funding. — [EA Forum: Open Phil seeking applications](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/HPdWWetJbv4z8eJEe/open-phil-is-seeking-applications-from-grantees-impacted-by); [The Deep Dive](https://thedeepdive.ca/future-fund-beneficiaries-still-scrambling-months-after-ftx-collapse); [DeepLearning.AI The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/how-ftxs-collapse-impacts-ai)
- FTX/Alameda was also Anthropic's largest Series B investor (about $500M). That stake was sold off in bankruptcy for about $1.3B (2024). — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/ip-law/ftx-to-sell-majority-of-anthropic-stake-for-884-million)
- **Slow fundraising:** Apollo began with a $1.4M first-year gap. Its one-year update said fundraising took more effort than expected, and at 18 months it still had "room for funding." — [Announcing Apollo](https://forum.nunosempere.com/posts/ysC6crBKhDBGZfob3/announcing-apollo-research); [Apollo 18-Month Update](https://www.apolloresearch.ai/blog/apollo-18-month-update/)
- **Funder concentration:** about 75% of Epoch's funding came from Open Philanthropy. — [Epoch Our Funding](https://epoch.ai/our-funding). Open Philanthropy has since been renamed Coefficient Giving. — [LongtermWiki grant record](https://www.longtermwiki.com/things/-1Afs6AJop)
- **Host-institution failure:** Oxford closed the Future of Humanity Institute on April 16, 2024 after "increasing administrative headwinds within the Faculty of Philosophy." Hiring and fundraising were frozen from 2020, and staff contracts were not renewed in late 2023. Bostrom called it "death by bureaucracy." — [Wikipedia: FHI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_of_Humanity_Institute); [Daily Nous (Apr 18, 2024)](https://dailynous.com/2024/04/18/end-future-of-humanity-institute/); [Asterisk](https://www.asteriskmag.com/issues/08/looking-back-at-the-future-of-humanity-institute)
- **Strategy churn and wind-down:** Conjecture raised VC money in 2022, pivoted to "Cognitive Emulation" in February 2023, drew public criticism of its research quality and leadership experience (EA Forum, 2023), and in March 2026 its CEO announced the project was "drawing to a close." — [EA Forum critique](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/gkfMLX4NWZdmpikto/critiques-of-prominent-ai-safety-labs-conjecture); [Wikipedia: Connor Leahy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Leahy)
- **Execution drag on volunteer projects:** EleutherAI reported months of delay from codebase problems, tooling friction (TF/TPU) and GPU shortages. — [EleutherAI retrospective](https://blog.eleuther.ai/year-one/)
- **Crowdfunding shortfall:** Transluce's Manifund page sought $2M and showed $40k raised at its January 2026 close. — [Manifund](https://manifund.org/projects/transluce-fund-scalable-democratic-oversight-of-ai)
- **Independence constraints:** METR refuses AI-company cash and frontier-lab employee donations to stay independent, which narrows its funding base. — [METR About](https://metr.org/about)

### Inferences
- A Colombian org should spread funding across philanthropic (Coefficient Giving, SFF, Manifund), government (UK AISI Alignment Project, possibly the Colombian state or multilaterals), corporate in-kind (compute and token donations) and earned revenue (evals and red-teaming contracts) from early on. Single-funder dependence is the most common risk in this sample.
- Do not embed the lab inside a university department as its only legal home; FHI shows the host can freeze hiring and fundraising. An independent nonprofit or fiscally sponsored entity (as Timaeus uses Ashgro), with university affiliations for researchers, is safer.
- If taking venture money, follow Gray Swan and Goodfire (a sellable product tied to a research credential) rather than Conjecture (venture money for a research agenda that then changed).
- Tell early funders up front if a PBC conversion is possible (as Apollo did) to avoid trust problems later.

### Gaps
- I found no systematic list of AI safety orgs that shut down or how many were affected by FTX. Most FTX-impact summaries come from secondary wikis.
- I did not find Conjecture's own March 2026 retrospective text; its reasons for closing are not confirmed beyond the Wikipedia summary.
- I found no documented failures of safety orgs in Latin America or the Global South, so lessons from those contexts are missing.
